STRAIGHT TALK — the part most sellers skip

Cracked... filled... cracked again —
because smearing over the surface never works

10cmREPAIR RANGE · hairline to serious void

Counterintuitive but true: big, wide voids are the easy part for FillerAce — the thick paste packs them without complaint. The repair people fumble most is the tiny one: the hairline crack. It looks so minor that most just smear a little filler over the top... and get the same ending every time: it peels, and the crack comes right back. It doesn't help that many everyday fillers cure hard with modest adhesion — one wall movement and they're done.

The reason is simple — a hairline groove is too narrow for any paste to reach the bottom of the wound. The smear only grips a paper-thin skin of surface, so the first wall movement or splash of water lifts the whole patch off. We won't sell you the "just smear it" dream. Here are the rules that make a repair actually last:

Rule 1 — Open the wound first

Hairline cracks must be cut wider with an angle grinder so the filler can reach the bottom of the crack — this step is the line between a repair that lasts years and one that peels in a month.

Rule 2 — Don't spread it thin

Scrape it too thin and the strength goes with it — keep at least normal filling thickness. The filler needs mass to carry load.

Straight talk on re-cracking

If the structure isn't vibrating or shifting hard, FillerAce prevents re-cracking very well — but if the building itself is still settling or moving heavily, no filler on Earth out-muscles a structure. That's a root-cause problem to fix first.

How much you need — estimate it yourself

Straight answer: "square metres" doesn't apply here

Filling isn't painting — usage depends purely on the width, depth and length of the groove. A quick way to estimate:

Filler (grams) ≈ groove width (mm) × depth (mm) × length (m) × 1.7

Example: a standard groove after cutting, 5 mm wide × 5 mm deep ≈ 40–45 g per metre — so a 1 kg tub covers roughly 20 metres of crack like that, waste included. For big voids, measure the actual volume on site — or send a photo with rough dimensions to our page chat and we'll estimate it free before you buy.

How to use

Everything you need is on this page — the heart of the job is opening the wound. Nail step one and the rest is easy.

  1. Open the wound — grind the crack wider

    Run an angle grinder along the crack to cut a groove wide and deep enough for the filler to reach the bottom — especially on hairline cracks, never skip this step. Smearing over the surface just peels off later. Dust and loose debris off when done.

    mandatory for hairline cracks

  2. Pack FillerAce into the groove

    Ready to use, no mixing — use a putty knife to press the filler in until the groove is full to the bottom, then strike it off clean. Remember rule 2: don't spread it thin — keep at least normal filling thickness. Big voids? Pack away; the paste won't sag.

    no mixingpack it full

  3. Smooth the surface with plain water

    A real job-site trick: while the paste is still damp, wipe the surface smooth with plain water — a well-wiped finish saves you a lot of sanding later. For extra-fine work, skim a finishing pass over the top.

    less sanding later

  4. Wait 2 hours, then sand

    Fast-drying — sand-ready in 2 hours. We recommend a power sander, or an angle grinder with a sanding disc, starting coarse then moving to fine grit for the smoothest finish, fastest.

    sand-ready in 2 hrscoarse → fine grit

  5. Finish the job — paint over

    Indoors: sand it, paint it, done. / Outdoors: we recommend a waterproof coat first, then paint for maximum service life — budget pick: SiliconePro, full-power pick: Exotic

    indoors: paint directlyoutdoors: waterproof first
⚠ Walls only — not recommended for floors: the filler surface can trap dirt, so on a walked-on floor it will go dark over time — with one exception: topcoat it with a PU-grade floor paint (it must flex with the filler). Not sure? Ask us on chat first.
