Plainly, before you pay: AquaShell 709 is engineered for vertical surfaces and nothing else — concrete, brick and block walls, inside or out. It is genuinely good at shedding driving rain, holding back damp and cutting down staining, at a price that doesn't make you wince. What it is not good at is abrasion. That single fact draws its boundary.

So: don't put it on floors people walk on or vehicles drive over, and don't put it in a swimming pool. It isn't that good, and we'd rather say so than sell you the wrong can. Permanent immersion is its own discipline, and we already make products built for it. Cheap product in the wrong arena costs you the money and the weekend.

The iron rule

Never add water to stretch it. The system is tuned in the jug. If it's genuinely too thick to lay down, a small amount of water to adjust viscosity is acceptable — thinning it to get more coverage is not. Thin it hard and you get a thinner film and worse waterproofing. Simple as that.

Accept this bit

It goes on milky white and dries clear. While you're rolling, the wall looks hazy and white — that disappears entirely as it dries, and what remains is a slight sheen. A matt wall will look glossier and a shade deeper once coated. Plenty of people like it — the wall reads as new. If a matt finish matters to you, test a hidden corner first, always.

How to use

Every number you need is on this page — no separate manual to hunt down. Two things carry the whole job: the surface must be genuinely dry and clean, and you must do both coats. Get those right and the wall lasts.

  1. Clean the surface and let it dry properly

    The wall must be dry and free of dust, oil, salt bloom (that white powder pushing out of the render) and general grime. If there's efflorescence or algae, scrub it all off and wait until it's bone dry. Otherwise the coating bonds to the dirt, not to your wall.

    100% dry surface

  2. Fill cracks and voids first

    The fixed rule of every coating job: open the crack out with a grinder, then fill it flush with a proper repair compound such as FillerAce, let it cure, then coat over it. A waterproofer is not a filler — smear it across a live crack and the crack simply comes back.

    Fill first → then coat

  3. Stir it properly

    Stir from the bottom of the container upward until the whole batch is uniform — don't swirl the top and start rolling. Do not add water or anything else, with the single exception of a little water if it's genuinely too thick to apply.

    Stir from the bottom

  4. First coat — thin and even

    Short-nap roller, brush or spray — all fine. Lay it on thin and even; don't force a thick film. Bare render and block will drink the first coat, and that's normal — that's the product going where it's supposed to go. / It also goes on milky white — equally nothing to worry about. Once fully dry it turns clear on its own, leaving only the sheen behind.

    Short-nap roller / brush / sprayKeep coat 1 thinOn white → dries clear

  5. Wait 2–4 hours, then second coat

    Let the first layer dry for roughly 2–4 hours, then apply the second. Two coats is the standard here, not an upsell — the coverage figures in the price table already assume both.

    2–4 h between coats2 coats = standard

  6. Let it cure 3–7 days

    After the final coat the film keeps developing for several more days. Give it 3–7 days to reach full properties — and during that window, no scrubbing, no pressure washing, nothing leaning against the wall.

    Full properties in 3–7 days

  7. Want to paint over it? Go ahead

    If the plan is to use it as a waterproof primer, let it dry fully and then paint as normal — you end up with fresh colour and a waterproof layer underneath it. Happy with the clear satin look? Then you're already finished.

    Primer → paint over it
⚠ Vertical surfaces only: this product is not recommended for horizontal surfaces — it will not take heavy abrasion. For floors with foot or vehicle traffic, use a proper floor coating system instead. And never use it in a swimming pool: permanent immersion needs PoolArmour.
Weather while you work: apply in dry conditions, above 10°C — a water-based system needs time for the water to leave the film. Don't start ahead of rain, and don't coat a wall still damp from yesterday's downpour. Wash tools with water immediately after use, while the material is still wet — once it's dry, cleaning gets a lot harder.
