Genuinely quick to use — but read these 4 rules before you open the jars

1:1equal parts A + B — the one rule you can't break

Almost impossible to get wrong — but these four rules are the line between a rock-hard repair and a soft blob that never sets. They take thirty seconds. Read them.

Scoop 1:1, knead until the streaks are gone

Take equal amounts of A and B and knead until not a single two-tone streak is left — any unmixed spot stays soft forever, no matter how long you wait.

Mix only what you'll use — never the whole jar

The clock starts the moment it's kneaded. Prep the surface first, then mix just enough for that round — leftovers are money in the bin. Close the lids tight, and never let a tool with A on it dip into jar B.

Dry, clean, grease-free surface

Sand off loose material, rust and flaking paint, then wipe away dust and oil — a clean, roughened surface is adhesion's best friend.

Gloves on, every time

It's a two-part chemical: rubber gloves are cheap insurance. If it gets on your skin, wash with soap and water right away — don't let it cure on you.

How to use — 5 steps, all on this page

Read this once and get to work — no manual hunting. One rule matters: 1:1 ratio, kneaded all the way through.

  1. Prep the surface before opening the jars

    Sand off loose material, rust and flaking paint; wipe away dust, oil and water until bone-dry — on pipe work, always shut off the water first. Glossy surfaces grip far better after a quick scuff with sandpaper.

    dry & cleanpipes = water off first

  2. Gloves on, then scoop A : B = 1 : 1

    Take equal amounts from jar A and jar B — eyeballing is fine. Scoop only what you'll use this round, with a separate tool for each jar so the two sides never cross-contaminate.

    1 : 1gloves onmix per round

  3. Knead until it's one uniform color

    Knead, twist, fold, repeat until the whole lump is one even color — see a streak, keep kneading. Unmixed spots never harden.

    one color = ready

  4. Shape, pack and press — done within 60 minutes

    Pack the putty into the hole or crack firmly, slightly proud of the edges, pressing out air pockets — on pipes, wrap well past the leak on all sides. Smooth the surface with a wet finger before it starts to set.

    work within 60 minpack tight, no air

  5. Let it cure, then sand, drill or paint

    It firms up to the touch in 3–4 hours and reaches full hardness at 24 hours — anything load-bearing, screwed into, drilled, or back under water pressure waits the full 24. Cold weather slows the cure.

    firm in 3–4 hfull cure 24 h
⚠ Know the limits: built for spot repairs, not wide-area coating · on high-pressure lines, treat it as a time-buying temporary fix and have the root cause repaired properly · keep away from children and wash your hands after every use.
