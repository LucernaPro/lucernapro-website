Why Your Bathroom Keeps Leaking No Matter How Many Times It's Been Fixed

Published Aug 2026 · by the LucernaPro team

Most homeowners assume a leaking bathroom means the tiles have failed. But dense ceramic tile is already an excellent water barrier as long as it isn't cracked. The point that actually loses, almost every time, is the grout line between the tiles — and if you repair without understanding this mechanism, the leak keeps circling back to the exact same spot.

This case digs into why grout is the true weak point, then matches the repair to what your bathroom is actually showing — from tiles still in perfect shape leaking only along the joints, all the way to a new-build bathroom that hasn't been tiled yet.

01The real problem isn't the tile — it's the grout line

Ordinary cement grout — even the off-the-shelf "waterproof" formulas — is still a cementitious material, and cementitious means porous. Under continuous water exposure, like a bathroom floor where water sits regularly, it behaves like a sponge: drawing water through those pores and letting it accumulate under the tiles, little by little, over months and years. That is the real cause of those seeping stains — not cracked tile.

The repair pattern we see fail on repeat: rip out all the tiles → apply a waterproofing layer never designed to withstand continuous ponding water → tile over it → finish the new joints with ordinary grout that doesn't actually seal. The result: the grout — the point where water really enters — is porous all over again. Within a year or two the problem is back where it started, right after paying to demolish the entire floor. And if the waterproofing layer underneath can't handle ponding water, it slowly degrades until nothing is left — the day stains appear on the ceiling below is the day this problem has already spread.

02Why you must always fill the grout full before coating over

Whatever product you coat over the existing tiles with, there is one iron rule you cannot skip: always repair and fill every grout line full before applying the coating — skip this step and the waterproofing system will not work.

The mechanics: a waterproofing film needs a continuous surface to bond to. Wherever a grout line is hollow or chipped, the film has to span that void with nothing underneath it. Add foot traffic and the building's normal daily movement, and that unsupported spot gets flexed until it tears — right at the joint. Filling the grout full first gives the film continuous support across the entire floor, so it works as a real water barrier instead of a membrane suspended over a void.

03Tiles still look great, leaking only along the joints — match the fix to the symptom

If your inspection shows no cracked tiles and the seepage comes purely from degraded grout lines, you don't need to remove a single tile — replacing the grout itself is enough.
