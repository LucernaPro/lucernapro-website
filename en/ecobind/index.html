EcoBind Gravel Lock
Pour it over the gravel — stones stay put, stop scattering

A loose-gravel path looks great for exactly one day. Then people walk on it, stones scatter, get kicked into the lawn, and you are back out there raking it flat again. EcoBind is a waterbase polymer adhesive for locking loose gravel and stones in place. It flows down between the stones and bonds them where they touch — the path firms up, walking is comfortable, and you can sweep leaves without the gravel sliding along. Mix with water, sprinkle through a sieve or spray it on. No professional tools required.

Genuinely good at walkways — but know these 4 things before ordering

Walkwaysonly — not for vehicles

We sell things as they actually are. Read all four before you hit order, and there will be no surprises on site.

Walkways only — vehicles cannot drive on it

The bond strength is adhesive-grade stone locking. It carries foot traffic comfortably, but it is not a surface for wheel loads. If you want a stone floor built for heavy duty, see BoundGravel — the top stone binder in our range, and a different system from EcoBind.

This is NOT a "sand binder" — completely different thing

The sand binders on the market are designed to coat the fine jointing sand between paving blocks — a thin film to stop sand washing out and weeds coming up. Pour one over full-size gravel and the bond simply is not there; the stones come loose like before. People confuse the two and get burned regularly. EcoBind is a gravel-locking adhesive made for this exact job, and once cured it takes a direct hosing (proof clip below).

Full strength from the factory — you add the water on site

We do not pre-dilute it. Every kilo you pay for is pure adhesive, which is why it comes out very viscous. Before use, mix in water until it flows through a sieve or a spray nozzle comfortably (see how-to below).

It locks the gravel you laid — it does not build you a new floor

The result depends on your groundwork: the base must be well compacted, the stones dry and clean. If the ground underneath settles, the stones settle with it. The liquid bonds stones — it does not fix structural problems.

How to use it — everything is on this page

No professional tools, no fussy mixing ratios — just add water until it pours nicely. The whole game is groundwork and patience while it dries.

  1. Prepare the area

    The base under the gravel must be well compacted. Spread the gravel to an even depth, clear leaves and dust — the stones must be bone dry, so do not apply right after rain.

    Dry, clean stones · firm base

  2. Mix with water in a bucket first

    Full-strength it is very viscous and awkward to pour — mix in a little water until it flows through a sieve or a spray nozzle comfortably, and stir it in properly every time before use.

    Always mix with water first

  3. Sprinkle through a sieve, or spray

    Use a bowl or scoop with a sieve bottom (a kitchen sieve works) — scoop and sprinkle evenly across the area, and the liquid runs down to the contact points between stones by itself. A sprayer works too. Usage rate roughly 1 kg per 1 m².

    1 kg ≈ 1 m²

  4. Let it dry — no walking, no water

    For at least the first 24 hours, keep feet off it and watch out for rain and garden sprinklers — the curing window is the only time this stuff loses to water.

    First 24 h: no feet, no water

  5. Full strength — then hose away

    Fully hardened in 2–3 days. After that: walk on it, sweep it, hose it down freely — heavy rain is not a problem. Rinse tools with plain water immediately, while the liquid is still wet.

    Full cure 2–3 days
Easy tip: pour a small one-square-metre test corner first, check how it soaks in and how it looks dry, then commit to the full area — with this kind of product, testing first is always cheaper than fixing later.
