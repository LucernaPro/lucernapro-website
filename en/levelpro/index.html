Potholes and hollows, closed in a day

A floor that has spalled into pits, potholes and shallow hollows is not a job for a small tube of filler, and it is not a job cement will hold — because fresh cement never really bonds to old cement. It just sits on top, and a few months later it lifts off in sheets · LevelPro is a two-part resin that you mix with ordinary fine sand right there on site, giving you a repair that is stronger than cement at the same thickness and that actually grips the old slab.

And here is the honest part, up front: this product exists to repair spots. If you are thinking of using it to cover a whole floor, work out the cost per square metre first — you will change your mind on your own.

Why LevelPro

The whole idea behind this product is one word: volume. A pothole swallows far more material than a crack does, and hauling bags of ready-mixed repair compound around a yard is both expensive and exhausting · So we sell you the resin only. The heaviest and cheapest ingredient in the mix, you pick up on site.

01

Buy the resin, find the sand on site

No special aggregate, nothing imported — ordinary fine construction sand, properly dry, works exactly as it should · 1 kg of resin takes 1.5–2.5 kg of sand, which means the small box that arrives in the post turns into two to three times its own weight of repair material by the time it goes into the hole.

02

Stronger than cement at the same thickness

This is why it can fill a shallow pit when cement cannot — cement needs thickness before it has any real strength. Trowel it thin into a shallow hollow and it cracks and lifts, every time · LevelPro cures to a compressive strength several times higher than the concrete in an ordinary house slab, so you can fill a shallow pit without breaking it open deeper just to gain thickness.

03

No bonding primer step

A cement repair means brushing on a bonding agent, waiting for it to go tacky, then rushing to place the mortar before it skins over — this product deletes that whole step · Clean, sound and dry is the entire preparation. Then you fill. Fewer steps means fewer ways to get it wrong.

04

Back in service the same day

Walkable in 4–6 hours · full load at 24 hours — compare that with a cement repair you have to cure for a week · For car parks, driveways, warehouses and factory floors that cannot simply shut down, this is where the product saves real money, and it is not the price on the bottle.

0.15sq m is what 1 kg of resin fills at 10 mm deep

We sell this product and we think it is good — but we would rather you did not order it and then get angry with us about how fast it ran out. Read these five points to the end and you will know for yourself whether your job wants this one or a different product of ours.

Price it by the square metre and it stops making sense

That 0.15 above is not a scare tactic — a hole the size of your palm, a centimetre deep, already eats several hundred grams of resin · This product is excellent value used to repair spots, and indefensibly expensive used to cover an area · If what you actually want is "make the whole yard look new and stop it dusting", that is a job for floor paint, not for a filler — fill the holes with this, then paint over the whole slab. That is several times cheaper.

Once mixed, you have about half an hour

The clock starts the moment the two parts meet · Roughly 30 minutes is the ceiling, and on a hot day or in a large batch it will be noticeably shorter, because a big mass of material heats itself up and sets faster · The right way to work is mix only what you can place, not mix the whole tub and then wander from hole to hole — nobody ever looked foolish for mixing too little. The people who mix too much end up owning one very expensive rock.

Damp sand ruins the job, and you will not notice that day

This is the number one reason a repair comes out weaker than it should · Sand from a pile in the open always carries moisture, however dry it feels in your hand · That moisture sits between the sand grains and the resin, quietly removing the bond while the mix still looks completely normal — then a few months later the whole patch lifts out for no visible reason · Dry the sand properly first, and sieve out the clods and leaf litter while you are at it.

It is hard — it is not a flexible material

Hardness is the selling point, which means it was never made for a joint that moves back and forth all day · Structural expansion joints, a slab that is actively settling, or a metal sheet roof that expands and contracts with the sun — fill those and it will crack along the same line again · Roof work and lapped sheet joints go to SpackleFlex, and awkward joints on smartboard or slick surfaces go to DeepStick

For ordinary floor cracks we point you elsewhere

Said plainly so you do not buy the wrong product — line cracks, thin spalled surfaces, and floor preparation before paint or a waterproofing system all go to PatchPro, and only PatchPro. It comes ready to use, needs no sand, is far easier to work with, and is cheaper for small quantities · LevelPro starts winning when the volume gets serious — deep holes, wide hollows, or a whole yard of them · And for genuinely heavy industrial work such as road surfaces under constant truck traffic, step up to PMMA

How to use it — all on this page

No separate manual to open. The real numbers are all here.

  1. Knock out everything loose first

    The edge of a hole that looks like it is still attached has usually already let go, it just has not fallen out yet · Work around the hole with a hammer or chisel until only sound, solid concrete is left — skip this step and the material you place will be extremely strong, but it will be bonded to a crust that was on its way off anyway · Holes shallower than 5 mm should be ground out deeper with steep edges. Never leave a feathered edge that tapers away to nothing. remove all loose materialat least 5 mm deepsteep edges, no feathering

  2. Clean it out dry and bare

    Clear the debris, then vacuum or blow it out right down to the bottom of the hole — dust left behind is a layer that stops the repair bonding to the slab · Remove every trace of oil and grease (factory floors and car parks always have some) · It must be completely dry, with no standing water in the bottom · No bonding primer of any kind is required. vacuum to the bottomno oilbone dry

  3. Get the sand ready before you open anything

    Ordinary fine construction sand is fine — you do not need a special grade · Sieve out clods, stones and leaf litter, then dry it thoroughly — this matters far more than people expect, because damp sand removes the bond while the mix still looks perfectly normal · Measure it out and have it standing next to you first, because once the resin is mixed there is no time to go looking for sand. ordinary fine sandsieve firstcompletely dry, no exceptions

  4. Weigh A : B = 2 : 1, and mix that first

    Weigh it properly, do not judge it by eye — a ratio that is off gives you material that never fully hardens, which will not show today but will show in six months · Mix Part A and Part B together thoroughly for 2–3 minutes before any sand goes in, scraping the bottom and sides of the container until the colour is uniform with no streaks · Adding sand before the resin is properly combined leaves soft spots hidden inside the repair that you cannot see. 2:1 by weightcombine the resin first, always2–3 min, scrape the bottom

  5. Add sand gradually, 1.5–2.5 times

    1 kg of resin takes 1.5–2.5 kg of sand · Less sand gives a looser mix that flows easily — good for shallow holes and for trowelling flat, but it uses more resin · More sand gives a stiff, damp-mortar consistency that holds its shape — good for deep holes and edges you need to sculpt, but overdo it and the mix goes crumbly · The test is simple: every grain of sand must be wetted by resin until the whole mix looks glossy. The moment you see dry white patches of sand, you have added too much. 1.5–2.5× the resin weightadd it graduallyglossy all through

  6. Pack it down to the bottom

    Use a trowel to press the material hard down to the base of the hole, leaving no trapped air — any void you leave behind is a spot that will sink and crack later · Holes deeper than 25–30 mm should be filled in layers: compact the lower layer, then add the next one while the first is still soft · Fill it level with the surrounding surface or very slightly proud, never dished below it. compact to the basedeep holes in layers

  7. Finish the surface within about 30 minutes

    The first half hour is the valuable part — trowel it flush with the existing floor, tidy the edges, and wipe off anything that has gone outside the line while it is still soft · Once it hardens, sanding it back is genuinely miserable work: abrasive paper barely touches it, you end up with an angle grinder, and you risk damaging the surrounding slab · If you want a crisp edge, mask both sides with paper tape beforehand and peel it away before the material sets. finish it while softvery hard to sand later

  8. Wait, then wash your tools immediately

    Walkable at 4–6 hours · full load and coating systems on top at 24 hours · In cold weather, wait longer — nobody has ever damaged a repair by giving it too much time · The working temperature range is 10–35°C · And wash trowels and buckets with thinner the moment you finish — let it harden on a tool and that is the end of the tool. Old buckets and cheap tools you are happy to throw away make this a much calmer job. walkable 4–6 hrsfull load 24 hrsthinner, immediately

The mistakes that waste the most material — never use damp sand (the number one cause) / never add sand before Part A and Part B are properly combined / never judge the ratio by eye / never mix more than you can place in 30 minutes / never fill a hole that is still wet or still dusty / never thin the mix with solvent to make it easier to trowel — the strength you paid for goes out with it.

Why cement never finishes the job

The question we get most often is "can I not just mix some cement and fill it?" — you can, and you will be back filling the same hole again in a few months. Here is why.

Fresh cement does not bond to old cement

Cement mixed and placed into a hole does not become one material with the slab around it — it simply sits there · Then the traffic goes over it, the sun and rain cycle through it, and it lifts out in one piece, with the edges still looking perfectly tidy right up until the last day.

The other thing people rarely hear is that cement needs thickness before it is strong enough to carry anything · A spalled hollow only half a centimetre deep is therefore a job that cement has already lost before it starts — too thin to carry load, and too thin to hold on.

Once the holes are filled, decide how to finish

On a concrete rooftop that has to keep water out, bring the holes flush first, then go over the whole area with SiliconePro · For a car park, garage or warehouse that just needs to look new and stop dusting, go to ThermaGlaze · Always give it the full 24 hours before any system goes on top.

Do not fill a hole caused by a settling slab

If the hollow is there because the ground under the slab has dropped, rather than because the surface spalled, then whatever you put in will simply go down with it · The signs are a whole slab sitting at a different level from its neighbours, or long cracks running around that area · That case has to be dealt with underneath first.
