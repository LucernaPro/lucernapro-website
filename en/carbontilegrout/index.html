How to use

Everything you need, numbers included, on this page — gun-type grouting really has only two rules left: the joint underneath must be clean and tape first, always. The gun handles the rest.

  1. Rake out all the old grout

    Use a grout rake to remove every bit of the old cement grout down to the joint, then brush and wipe until clean and dry — new grout only holds as well as the joint under it is clean.

  2. Tape both sides of the joint

    Run masking tape along both sides of every joint — the iron rule from real job sites: no tape, no work. Grout that sets on the tile face is very hard to get off.

    NO TAPE = NO START

  3. Load the gun, purge the first squeeze

    Fit the dual cartridge into the gun, open the tip, then squeeze out and discard the first bit until both parts flow evenly — then start on the real joints.

    EVEN FLOW FIRST, THEN GO

  4. Gun the grout into the joint

    Apply between the tape lines and pack the joint full with no voids — an air pocket today is a water path tomorrow. Work zone by zone without long pauses.

  5. Tool the line smooth

    Use an ice-cream stick or any smooth-edged tool to press the grout down into the joint and strike a clean, even line end to end.

  6. Pull the tape immediately

    As each line is finished, pull the tape right away while the grout is still fresh — wait until it hardens and the edge tears ragged.

    PULL TAPE WHILE FRESH

  7. Wait 4–6 hours, back in service

    Let it set for 4–6 hours and the area is back in use — keep water off it while it cures.

    SET TIME 4–6 HRS
⚠ Remember: once compound has passed through the mixing tip the clock is running — plan continuous zones, do not leave the gun sitting mid-job, and wipe any smears off the tile face immediately while fresh.
