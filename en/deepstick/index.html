Why DeepStick

Ordinary fillers lose on two battlefields: surfaces that refuse to bond and joints that keep moving — DeepStick was born for exactly those two.

01

Bonds where adhesives give up

Slick surfaces, glossy glazed tile, metal, wood, foam, even plastic — adhesion so strong no surface abrasion is needed before filling. Exterior smartboard joints that defeat ordinary sealants? That is home turf for this one.

02

Works underwater

HydroCure Polymer chemistry — moisture is the catalyst, not the enemy. Plug leaks while water is still running, fix tiles underwater, repair wet jobs other materials must wait out.

03

Expands — never shrinks

Ordinary fillers dry, then sink, pull away from edges, need refilling — DeepStick flips it: it expands slightly as it cures, packing itself into the joint. Tight and full from the first pass.

04

Firm in the sweet spot — sand & paint it

Not brittle like rigid glues, not saggy like caulk — tough enough to take impact, flexible enough to move with the surface. Once cured: sand it, drill it, paint it like normal.

DeepStick is a two-part system — liquid resin + powder — mixed on site by you, with consistency tuned to the job: from a thin spreadable paste to a clay-like putty you can shape. That freedom comes with steps you genuinely have to follow. Mix it wrong and the job dies in the cup — so we tell you here, instead of letting you find out the hard way.

The chemistry inside is HydroCure Polymer technology powered by Covestro — it cures on moisture from the air and the substrate, which is why it works underwater. It is also why fresh work on damp concrete can show small blisters: that is the material expanding and packing itself in, not a defect. Press them flat while still tacky (the how is in the steps below).

Rule number one — no skipping

First time: mix the smallest amount you possibly can and test on a small area only — and watch the how-to video all the way through before real work. This matters.

Everyday jobs do not need the heavy weapon

Cracks on ordinary rendered walls — FillerAce is far easier and more convenient (it handles movement too). Save DeepStick for the brutal surfaces nothing else grips: wood, smartboard, slick finishes, plastic, underwater work.

How to use

Everything with real numbers, right on this page — the heart of it is mixing correctly and finishing while tacky. Master those two and the heavy chemistry does the rest.

  1. Prep the surface

    Surface must be clean — no dust, no oil films — but no abrasion needed; it grips slick and glazed surfaces as-is. Wet surfaces and underwater work are fine — moisture is this system’s accelerator.

  2. Mix — the make-or-break step

    Pour the resin you need into a container first, then sprinkle the powder into the resin a little at a time, stirring continuously until you reach the consistency you want — for filling work aim for a clay-like putty. Ratio by weight: powder at 1.5–2.5× the resin, tuned to the job. Never pour resin into powder (it clumps and will not mix through) and never mix big batches — the putty gets too stiff to stir. Mix only what you can place within ~30 minutes.

    Powder into resin — graduallyNever resin into powderPot life ~30 min

  3. Spray the trowel with Sonax, then go

    Before spreading, spray your trowel with Sonax — the paste will not stick to the tool and spreading gets far easier. Fill the joint and crack lines themselves; no need to skim the whole surface — saves a lot of material.

    Sonax on trowel = no sticking

  4. Finish while tacky — the biggest time-saver

    On concrete, which usually carries moisture, small blisters may appear as the work nears set — do not wait for full cure and sand them off; that wastes serious time. While still tacky, spray dish soap or Sonax onto a rubber-gloved hand and press-smooth in circles. Done at the right moment, one pass usually finishes it — nearly no sanding after. If the timing slips or the substrate is very damp, blisters can return; simply repeat the pass.

    Press-smooth while tacky

  5. Give it the full time

    Initial set in 6 hours / fully cured and in service at 24 hours — do not touch the work early. Heavy chemistry earns its keep by taking its time.

    Initial set 6 hrIn service 24 hr

  6. Sand, shape + paint over

    Once cured, sand, drill and shape it like normal — a sanding disc on an angle grinder speeds this up a lot. Always topcoat with paint when the job is done for long-term durability: this polymer surface can yellow under years of accumulated harsh sun, and the paint layer is its sunscreen.

    Topcoat = long life
