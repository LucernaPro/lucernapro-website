CorePrimer
Deep-penetrating primer that turns weak concrete into a solid base for PoolArmour

When pool paint peels, the paint is rarely the culprit — it's the concrete underneath that wasn't sound enough: old render, dusty laitance, a surface so thirsty the paint never gets a grip. CorePrimer exists for exactly that. A thin, clear liquid that soaks down into the concrete and cures inside it, locking a weak, chalky surface into one consolidated base before the paint ever lands — and it doesn't just prime, it bonds the concrete and the waterproofing topcoat into a single reinforced system. The adhesion you get isn't "it sticks"; it's "it won't let go".

Single component, ready to use from the can — and the part crews love most: wait about 10 minutes, then topcoat straight away. No hours between coats. A whole pool finished in one day.

Why CorePrimer

An ordinary primer is one more thin coat sitting on top of the concrete. This one is built the other way round: it goes to work inside the concrete instead of lying on it.

01

Deep penetration — locks a weak surface into a solid base

The liquid is deliberately thin so it soaks into the pores of the concrete and cures in there. A surface that is dusty, chalky or very absorbent gets consolidated into a firmer base — so the paint isn't gripping dust, it's gripping concrete that has been tightened up first.

02

Chemical anchor — concrete and waterproofing become one system

Topcoat while the primer is still tacky and the two layers bond chemically rather than just sitting on each other — the result is the kind of adhesion that heavy-use pools, public pools and pools submerged all year demand. That's why we call it the primer for jobs you cannot afford to redo.

03

Single component — no mixing ratio to get wrong

No Part B, nothing to weigh, no pot-life clock — open, stir gently, apply. Two-pack primers fail most often on a mis-measured ratio; this one removes that risk entirely.

04

10 minutes, then topcoat — the whole pool in one day

Most primers need hours to dry before paint, so a day buys you a single step. CorePrimer needs about 10 minutes to go tacky, then PoolArmour goes straight over it. Work zone by zone — primer down, paint follows — and a full pool is done in one day.

should also tell you what it's not for

10minutes is the window for the topcoat — speed is the strength, but you have to plan for it

This costs several times more than an ordinary primer because it works in a different way — we'd rather you spend the money only when your job needs it. Read these five before you transfer.

It's a concrete primer — not a tile primer

Where it wins: concrete, render and cement surfaces with pores to soak into · On glazed tile, polished stone or any smooth non-absorbent surface there is nowhere to penetrate, so it gives you no advantage — for a tiled pool, message us first and we'll point you at the right product.

The 10-minute window — miss it and you sand

PoolArmour must go on while the primer is still tacky to the touch. Let it dry to a slick gloss and the paint won't bond properly · If you miss the window, sand the surface until it's dull, then re-prime a thin coat — the way to never miss it is to prime only as much as you can topcoat in time, not the whole pool first.

Sound concrete doesn't need it

PoolArmour already goes directly onto solid concrete — this is for old render, dusty or chalky surfaces, very absorbent concrete, heavy-use pools, or jobs that cannot fail. If a firm rub leaves no powder on your palm and it's an ordinary home pool, keep the money.

Damp is fine — standing water is not

It cures with atmospheric moisture, so it handles a damp concrete surface better than ordinary primers — but "damp" does not mean "wet". Never apply over puddles or a glossy-wet surface: where water pools, the film foams and doesn't penetrate. Blot to damp first, always.

Solvent-based — mind flames and fumes

To penetrate, the liquid has to be thin, which means it contains solvent and is flammable. In a pool with poor air movement you need a fan running. No smoking, no open flames, no spark-producing tools in the work area. Gloves and eye protection every time — this is not a product for an unventilated enclosed space.

How to use — everything on this page

All the real numbers are here — the job is not hard, but there is a stopwatch running at step 5. Read to the end before you open the tin.

  1. Check the concrete — new concrete must be fully cured

    New concrete or render needs at least 4 weeks of curing before you start · On old concrete, rub hard with your palm: powder on the hand = a weak surface = this product's home ground · Cracks and holes must be repaired first with PatchPro — a primer is not a crack filler. New concrete: wait 4 weeksFix cracks first

  2. Clean it — algae, residue and flaking old paint

    Scrub off algae, oil, pool-chemical residue and any old paint that is blistered or peeling, right down to the concrete (BioShield helps with algae) · Let it go damp — a damp surface is fine, but no puddles and no glossy-wet areas. Blot standing water away first. No algae, no oilDamp OK, no puddles

  3. Plan your zones — there is a 10-minute window

    Before opening the tin, divide the pool into zones of roughly 5–10 m² — as much as your crew can topcoat within 10 minutes · Have PoolArmour stirred and ready, roller and cutting-in brush in hand — never prime the whole pool and then open the paint. That is the one mistake that wastes this product. Zones of 5–10 m²Paint ready before priming

  4. Prime once, thin — let it soak, don't let it pool

    Open, stir gently, no power mixing, and apply a thin, even coat with a brush or short-nap roller. The aim is to get it into the concrete, not to leave a gloss on top · One coat is enough — where it soaks in and goes matt immediately, add a little more just there, no second pass over everything · Never leave puddles or a thick film — anything that doesn't soak in will foam and become a weak layer under the paint. 1 kg ≈ 10 m²One coat onlyThin and soaked in

  5. Wait about 10 minutes — tacky, then paint immediately

    Wait for the surface to change from glossy-wet to tacky — touch it and it pulls slightly at your finger. Usually around 10 minutes, faster in hot weather · Roll the first coat of PoolArmour straight over it while it's still tacky, following the steps on the PoolArmour page — the two layers bond into one system. Wait ~10 minTopcoat while tacky

  6. Missed the window — sand and re-prime

    If the primer has dried to a slick gloss and the paint isn't on yet, don't push on · Sand until the gloss is gone, wipe off the dust, and re-prime a thin coat, then wait 10 minutes and get the paint on in time this round — a little wasted primer beats a wasted pool. Dried glossy = sand

  7. Next zone — leapfrog until the pool is done

    Prime the next zone, running it continuously into the previous edge without a gap, then paint behind it as before · The second coat of PoolArmour goes on 2–3 hours after the first, as usual — the whole pool finishes in one day. Wait the paint's full cure time before filling. Zone by zoneDone in one day

  8. Clean tools with thinner straight away

    This is a solvent-based product that cures on contact with moisture — wash brushes and rollers in thinner before it dries, or it will set hard in the tools · Close an opened tin tightly and immediately: humidity in the air is what cures it, so a tin left open thickens and skins over. Best used up within one job.

The mistakes that waste it most often — never prime the whole pool and then go looking for the paint: the window is 10 minutes / never apply over puddles or a glossy-wet surface — damp yes, wet no / never apply thick or leave it pooled: what doesn't soak in is a weak layer under the paint / never use it on glazed tile — nowhere to penetrate / no smoking or open flames in the work area — the liquid is flammable.
Safety — a pool is a low, enclosed space where vapour collects: keep a fan moving air the whole time. Wear gloves, eye protection and an organic-vapour mask · Storage — lid tight, cool and shaded, away from heat and flames. Never leave the tin in the sun.
