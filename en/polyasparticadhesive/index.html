Dual-cartridge glue that sets in minutes

Why Polyaspartic

There is no shortage of fast-setting glue on the market. The catch is that most of it buys speed with something else — a smell that rules out working in a closed room, a cartridge you have to race through once it's open, and a finish that goes chalky the moment it sees sunlight. This chemistry was picked so you don't pay any of those.

01

Fast, but you choose how fast

We make it in two versions so you can match the job — a 1-minute version for single spots where the position is already certain, and a 3-minute version for work that needs lining up. We recommend the 3-minute one as the default; it is by far the safer choice on a real site.

02

Mix small batches — no need to use the whole cartridge

With a fast-setting adhesive this matters enormously: you never have to mix the whole cartridge at once. Dispense what that one spot needs, mix only that, and the rest stays in the cartridge for next time. / One cartridge covers several jobs across several days. It isn't something you open and then have to race through.

03

Barely any smell — work indoors

Ordinary two-part adhesives carry a sharp odour that makes an enclosed space almost unworkable. This one is very low odour, which puts occupied interiors, hotels, hospitals and lived-in homes back on the table — repairs during business hours, nobody has to move out. (Low odour is not the same as no ventilation needed — keep the air moving anyway.)

04

It can go outside

This is where Polyaspartic chemistry earns its place. It's a family known for standing up to sunlight and UV without yellowing or going brittle. Plenty of fast-set adhesives are perfectly good indoors and then turn chalky and crumbly after a few months in the open. This one goes outdoors — pergolas, balcony work, signage, repairs beyond the eaves.

Pick the right one — we make two

Both versions cost the same. What differs is the mix ratio, and that is what sets how much time you get after mixing. If you're unsure, take the three-minute one — that's the one we recommend as standard, because it is the safer choice on a real site. / You never measure the ratio yourself — the cartridge is built to dispense the correct proportions every time you squeeze both barrels together.

STRAIGHT TALK — what we'll tell you before you pay

It really is fast. What it is not is forgiving of anyone who didn't prepare.
Four things, up front.

4 hrFULL CURE — BEFORE FULL LOAD

One — this dual cartridge has no mixing nozzle. You dispense and mix it yourself. We'd rather say that in the first line than have you find out on site. Dual cartridges come both ways, with a static mixer and without, and ours is without. You squeeze part A and part B onto a mixing board or a small cup and stir it yourself for 15–20 seconds before use. The upside is real: you control the batch size, you mix exactly what the job needs, and you're not throwing away a nozzle full of set glue every time you stop. The downside is equally real: it has to be mixed thoroughly. A poorly mixed patch turns into a soft spot you won't discover until it lets go. / The ratio itself is not something to worry about — the cartridge handles that, so squeezing both barrels gives you the right proportions. The effort you supply is the stirring, not the measuring.

Two — one minute means one minute. That isn't marketing copy. The clock starts the moment A meets B, not when you start spreading — and the 15–20 second stir has already eaten a quarter of it. So everything gets laid out before you dispense: surface prepped, piece waiting, stirrer in hand, rag beside you. Nine out of ten people who have a bad time with this glue lost it right here, not to the product.

Three — you can mix in small batches. You are not committed to the whole cartridge. This one gets misunderstood a lot, and it stops people buying: opening the cartridge does not start a race. Dispense what that spot needs, mix only that, and the rest sits in the cartridge for next time. / The flip side still holds: don't mix a generous pile and work through it slowly, because the remainder will go off before you get to it. Used in small batches, a 50 ml cartridge covers far more ground than people expect.

Four — wipe the cartridge tips the second you finish. Every time. This sounds fussy and it's purely about money: glue left sitting at the nozzle hardens and plugs it, and next time you reach for it nothing comes out while half a cartridge sits trapped inside. Two passes with a dry rag, cap on firmly, done.

How to use it

Every number you need is on this page — read it through before you squeeze the first cartridge. It isn't difficult, but it moves fast enough that there's no coming back to read the next step halfway through.

  1. Choose the version first

    Single spot, position already certain → the 1-minute version (mixed 1:1). / Several spots, larger piece, needs lining up → the 3-minute version (mixed 2:1). / Can't decide? Take the 3-minute one — it's the one we recommend as standard.

    1:1 = 1 min2:1 = 3 min (recommended)

  2. Get the surface dry, clean and oil-free

    Both faces must be properly dry and free of dust, oil, grease and wax — brush it down, then wipe it again. Every adhesive on earth fails on a dirty surface, and this one moves too fast to give you a second chance at it.

    dry · clean · oil-free

  3. Glossy surface? Key it first

    Anything smooth enough that there's nothing for the glue to bite into — glazed tile, shiny plastic, an already-lacquered face — wants a rub with 120–180 grit until the shine is gone, then a thorough dust-off.

    120–180 grit until dull

  4. Lay everything out — before you dispense

    This is the step people skip and then lose a batch over: mixing board or small cup · stirrer · dry rag for the nozzle tips · the piece you're bonding · a clamp if you're using one. All of it within reach. Don't dispense until it is, because the clock starts the instant A meets B.

    everything within reach first

  5. Dispense A and B — equal amounts

    Squeeze both barrels together onto the board — nothing to measure, nothing to calculate. The cartridge is built to deliver the right proportions on its own (1-minute version = A 1 : B 1, 3-minute version = A 2 : B 1). All you do is press both barrels evenly. / Dispense only what that spot needs — there is no need to mix the whole cartridge. The rest stays in the cartridge for next time. / The sealed dual cartridge keeps A and B apart until you want them together and keeps moisture out, so cap it properly the moment you're done.

    squeeze together, no measuring1 min = A1:B13 min = A2:B1

  6. Stir for 15–20 seconds

    Work quickly but work it through — fold from the edge into the centre, turn the stirrer over, scrape the bottom of the cup. Keep going until it's one even colour with no streaks. / An under-mixed patch becomes a spot that never properly hardens, and you won't find out until it releases.

    15–20 secondsscrape the bottom too

  7. Spread and join immediately — clock runs from the mix

    A thin layer across the whole contact face beats a thick blob every time. Then bring the parts together — the 1:1 version must be finished inside 1 minute, the 2:1 inside 3, counted from when you started mixing, not from when you started spreading. / Past that window, don't push your luck. Glue that has begun to skin won't bond properly. Bin it and mix fresh; it's cheaper than doing the job twice.

    1:1 ≤ 1 min2:1 ≤ 3 min

  8. Press it home, then let go

    Once joined, press firmly to squeeze out any trapped air between the faces and hold until you feel it grab. That's the whole point of this product — you don't stand there any longer. Vertical work needs no tape and no propping stick.

    press out trapped airthen let go

  9. Wipe the tips and clear up — right now

    Wipe both cartridge tips with a dry rag the moment you finish dispensing, then cap firmly, or next time nothing comes out. / The stirrer and mixing board with glue on them: bin them. Don't save them for reuse. / Cured glue is extremely difficult to remove, so keep it off anything you'd regret in the first place.

    wipe the tips every time

  10. Wait 4 hours before real use

    Being able to let go after a minute means it's holding position — not that it's at full strength. Give it the full 4 hours before it takes real load, before you sand or paint over it, and before you let it meet water or direct sun. / Bonding something and putting it straight to work is the most common failure we hear about, and the glue isn't what went wrong.

    4 hours = full cure

  11. Storage

    Keep it cool, dry and out of the sun, away from damp. Always cap it firmly — a sealed dual cartridge only works while it's actually sealed.

    capped tight · out of the sun
⚠ Don't use it to hold heavy things — we'll say this plainly: this adhesive was built for small bonds, repairs and fast assembly work. It is not a glue for hanging or holding heavy items — loaded shelving, wall cabinets, grab rails, anything a person leans or pulls on, or anything that hurts someone if it falls. Always use mechanical fixings — plugs and screws. Use the adhesive alongside them if you like, but never let it carry the weight on its own. / We haven't published a bond-strength figure because we don't yet have test results we'd stand behind — while there is no number, treat it as unable to carry load. That's the safest assumption for you.
⚠ Three jobs to ask us about before ordering: permanently submerged joints · anything in direct contact with food · structural bonds carrying a person's weight. We don't sell blind on these three — describe the job first and we'll tell you honestly whether it fits, and point you elsewhere if something else suits it better.
⚠ Basic precautions: wear gloves while working, work somewhere with decent airflow (low odour doesn't mean ventilation is optional), and avoid eye contact. If it does reach the eyes, rinse immediately with plenty of clean water and seek medical attention. Keep out of reach of children and don't use it for anything it wasn't made for.
Site temperature changes the numbers: on a very hot day the reaction runs faster and your working window will be shorter than the figures on this page — mix smaller batches than usual and move quicker. On a cool day it slows slightly, so allow more than 4 hours before putting it under load. If you're unsure, mix one small test batch and time it once. That costs a great deal less than losing the real job.
