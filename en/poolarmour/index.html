STRAIGHT TALK

Concrete pools are brutal territory —
honest truth before we sell you anything: peeling is possible

24/7UNDERWATER DUTY · CONTINUOUS SUBMERSION

Concrete is unpredictable — unlike steel, which behaves. Every pool shell hides its own variables: invisible moisture trapped beneath the surface, alkalinity that reacts with coatings, and laitance — a dusty cement skin that stops paint from reaching the real concrete.

And one thing we cannot stress enough: concrete that looks solid often is not. The best paint in the world peels if the surface underneath is not ready. So we do not sell the fantasy that "it sticks no matter what" — we hand you the full toolkit: two primers in the same family, and a way to prove the chemistry before it touches your pool (see the tile-back test below).

The iron rule of the system

Apply directly onto concrete — never sandwich in primers or liquids from outside our system. A foreign layer under the film is a peeling time-bomb.

Pool Primer — budget pick

Single-component pool primer, 5 kg / 700 THB, covers 25–50 m² — light on budget, strong on adhesion. For older concrete you are not quite sure about.

CorePrimer — god-tier

5 kg / 2,690 THB, roughly 50 m² in a single coat — a deep-penetrating primer that consolidates weak, dusty concrete and anchors the paint to it as one system, ready for topcoat in 10 minutes. For heavy-duty pools or risky substrates where failure is not an option.

How to use

Everything is on this page, every number included — pool work comes down to surface prep and patience during cure. Nail those two and the film lasts for years.

  1. Clean and prep the surface

    The surface must be clean, dry, free of dust, algae and oil — scrape off any old paint that is blistered or peeling. The new film only bonds as well as the surface beneath it.

  2. Fill every crack first — always

    The fixed rule of every coating system: fill cracks and holes flush before painting over. Skip this and no paint on earth will keep the water out.

  3. Assess the concrete — prime only when needed

    Sound, solid concrete: apply PoolArmour directly, no primer needed / aged, degraded concrete or heavy-duty pools: prime first with Pool Primer (budget) or CorePrimer (deep-penetrating, topcoat in 10 minutes) — never use primers or liquids from outside our system.

    Direct onto concreteOur primers only

  4. Stir it properly

    One component, no mixing, no measuring — but before you paint, stir the bucket thoroughly until fully uniform. Do not get lazy here: what settles at the bottom is part of the formula.

    No A:B mixingNo pot-life countdown

  5. First coat — keep it thin

    Roller, brush or sprayer, whatever suits you — and here is the key trick: concrete drinks paint, so keep the first coat thin. Let it seal the surface, then lay the full body down in coat two. Keep the film continuous with no gaps, and catch every corner and joint.

    First coat: thinAll colors: 1 kg ≈ 5 m² (2 coats)

  6. Wait 2–3 hrs, then coat two

    Let the first coat dry for around 2–3 hours, then apply the second — a minimum of 2 coats for full waterproofing performance.

    Recoat: 2–3 hrsMinimum 2 coats

  7. Let it cure — then fill

    Touch-dry in 1–2 hours / full cure in 24–48 hours / and the rule that punishes impatience hardest: do not fill the pool for 3–5 days — give the film time to reach full adhesion and waterproofing. A few patient days buys you years of pool.

    Touch-dry 1–2 hrsFull cure 24–48 hrsFill after 3–5 days
⚠ Weather: avoid painting in intense midday sun or rain — and when working deep inside a pool shell (a natural air trap), keep the area well ventilated.
Water care after painting — free lifespan extension: always dissolve chlorine in water first, never toss tablets or pour powder straight in (wherever concentrated chlorine sits is where paint gets eaten), and keep pH between 7.0–7.8 — let the water run acidic often enough and any paint will fail, not just ours.

PRE-APPLICATION TEST

Test on the "back of a tile" before the real pool

We recommend every customer run this test before buying the big lot — because every pool hides three variables you cannot control:

Moisture Content

Hidden moisture

Invisible dampness or hydrostatic pressure beneath the surface — pushing the film up from below.

Alkalinity

Concrete alkalinity

High pH in the cement can react with the coating and disrupt bonding at the chemical level.

Laitance

Cement-dust skin

A weak dusty layer floating on the surface — paint grips the dust, never the real concrete.

The answer: test on the unglazed back of a ceramic tile — porosity closest to raw concrete, yet the most stable, trustworthy surface there is. No hidden moisture, no alkalinity interference = a miniature lab that proves the coating chemistry and nothing else.

1

Prepare

One spare tile — wipe the unglazed back clean, dust-free and bone dry.

2

Apply

Paint PoolArmour on the tile back following the normal steps above (2 coats, 2–3 hrs between).

3

Full cure

Let it cure fully, 24–48 hours — rush this and the test result means nothing.

4

Immerse

Submerge the whole tile and leave it — the paint must hold tight: no blistering, no peeling.

The logic of the test: pass on the tile back = the chemistry and formulation work, full stop — so if blistering or peeling appears in the real pool later, the root cause is instantly clear: surface preparation or the site concrete condition. You fix the actual problem instead of guessing. This tiny test is the cheapest insurance in the whole project.
