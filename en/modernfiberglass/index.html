STRAIGHT TALK — THE TRUTH UP FRONT

Cures slow — at least 6 hours.
And that's exactly the point.

6+ HRSLOW CURE · BY DESIGN

Ordinary resins give you minutes — mix, then race a cup that's already heating up. Lay the cloth crooked and it's too late. That's where beginners lose. Modern Fiberglass goes the other way: slow cure, 6 hours minimum. Misplace the cloth? Reposition it. Missed an air bubble? Come back and roll it out. Laminate a whole hull without mixing tiny cup after tiny cup.

More straight talk: this product gives you "tough", not "glass-hard" — the hardest materials are usually the most brittle, and we picked the side that takes impact and movement without snapping. If you need the part back in service within the hour, this isn't your product. If you want a repair that holds for years, you're in the right place.

How to use

Everything you need is on this page — the heart of fibreglass work is a clean, dry surface and wetting the cloth out properly. Nail those two and the repair lasts

  1. Surface prep — the step that matters most

    Clean thoroughly: remove dust, oil, algae and any loose material — the surface must be completely dry for full adhesion / Deep cracks or voids? Fill them level first, always, then lay the cloth system over the top

    Clean + bone dryFill deep damage first

  2. Cut the cloth to overlap the damage

    Cut the woven cloth so it extends well beyond the damaged area on all sides — on curves and corners the weave presses neatly into shape

  3. Prime the surface with resin, then lay the cloth

    A trick straight from real job sites: brush a thin coat of resin onto the surface first, then lay the cloth into the wet film — it grabs and stays put, easy to position, no sliding around

    Resin first → then cloth

  4. Work resin through until translucent

    Brush and press resin through the weave until the cloth is saturated — the tell is unmistakable: the cloth turns translucent when fully wetted out. Press out all air; edges must lie flat, never lifted

    Translucent = wetted outPress out all air

  5. Want maximum durability — go two layers

    For hard-loaded repairs or spots that hurt when they fail, lay a second cloth + resin layer — a two-layer laminate leaves durability worries behind

    Heavy duty: 2 layers

  6. Let it cure — no shortcuts

    Leave the system to cure for at least 6 hours (cool weather or thick builds may take up to 12) — the resin locks the glass weave into a tough, highly flexible protective shell

    Cure 6–12 h1 kg pairs with 1 m² cloth
⚠ Safety: Wear gloves and avoid skin contact with uncured resin. Work with good ventilation, reseal the tin immediately after use (the resin reacts with moisture in the air) and keep away from children.
