2:1MIX RATIO · WEIGH IT, NEVER EYEBALL IT

Straight talk, same as every page we write: concrete is unpredictable — hidden moisture, alkalinity and surface laitance lurk in every cement pond, and no coating on earth controls them one hundred percent.

As for tiled ponds — glossy glaze is the enemy of every coating ever made. Our advice, no sugar-coating: sand the tiles rough before painting, and rougher is better, for the long-term safety of the film. PondMax adhesion is very good but not PolyPro-level — that's our adhesion king. If your project truly cannot fail, skip straight to PolyPro. No hard feelings.

Concrete ponds

Substrate must be sound, not crumbling; always fill cracks completely first — damp is fine, but no standing water during application.

Tiled ponds

Always sand to a rough profile first — factory glaze is too slick to trust a film's life to. Trade a bit of sanding for certainty. Worth it every time.

2:1 — no shortcuts

Two-part system, 2 parts A : 1 part B by weight — get it wrong and the coating never dries. And once mixed you have about 15 minutes. Mix only what you can use.

How to use

Everything you need is on this page — a two-part polymer has two golden rules: weigh it exactly and work inside the clock. The system handles the rest.

  1. Prepare the surface

    The surface must be clean, sound, free of dust, algae and oil — damp or matt-wet is fine, but absolutely no standing water / Tiled ponds: always sand rough first — the rougher, the surer.

    Damp OK · puddles notTile: sand rough first

  2. Fill every crack first — always

    The iron rule of every coating system: fill cracks and holes completely flush first, then coat over — skip this and the best coating in the world won't seal the pond.

  3. Gear up before opening the cans

    Before it cures, Part B is a skin and eye irritant — wear gloves and eye protection every time you mix and apply (once fully cured it's safe for humans and fish alike).

    Gloves + eye protection

  4. Weigh 2:1 and mix like you mean it

    Mix 2 parts A : 1 part B by weight — use a scale, never eyeball it. Then stir until it is truly one homogeneous mix, scraping the bottom and sides of the bucket. Don't get lazy here: poorly mixed material is exactly where the coating never dries.

    A : B = 2 : 1 by weightMix only what you'll use in 15 min

  5. First coat — race the clock

    Once mixed you have ~15 minutes of working time — get the roller or brush moving immediately, work forward in lanes for a continuous film, and catch every corner, joint and pipe collar. Never mix the whole pond's worth at once — mix batch by batch, only as much as you can lay down in time.

    Pot life ~15 min

  6. Wait ~6 hrs, then second coat

    Let the first coat dry for around 6 hours, then apply the second — 2 coats is the standard system (1 kg ≈ 5 m² is already the full two-coat figure).

    Recoat ~6 hrsStandard: 2 coats

  7. Let it cure — then release the fish

    We recommend a full cure of 2–3 days, one rinse, then fill and release the fish — in a genuine hurry, once the film is completely dry you can fill: there's nothing fish-toxic in it, unlike bare cement ponds. But 2–3 patient days is a gift of full strength to the film.

    Full cure 2–3 daysRinse before final fill
⚠ Remember: the clock starts the moment you mix — never mix more than you can apply; material that sets in the bucket is money in the bin. And don't paint when rain is coming: an uncured film caught by rain means starting over.
Why it works on damp surfaces: PondMax's curing system is moisture-tolerant technology from Huntsman Advanced Materials — the same chemistry family used in marine coatings and wet-concrete structural repair, so it bonds even while the surface is still damp, no primer required.
