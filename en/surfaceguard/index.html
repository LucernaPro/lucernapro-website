SurfaceGuard
Water-based concrete floor paint for indoors and out

Most floor paints that are genuinely durable come with a smell strong enough to empty the house for a day — SurfaceGuard was made to close that gap. Built on water-based Polyurethane Dispersion (PUD) technology, the film you get is real polyurethane — water-resistant, resistant to the alkali in concrete, resistant to everyday scuffs and scratches — but the main ingredient is water, not thinner, so the odour is very low and you can paint a room with people in it. Single component: open, stir, paint. Wash the roller in plain water when you're done.

And we'll say this before you pay: it is not a factory floor paint — forklifts and regular chemical spills belong to another system. Read the STRAIGHT TALK section to the end before deciding.

STRAIGHT TALK — read before you pay

We sell this product
but we'll tell you fully what it isn't

7days is what the film needs to reach full strength

The market is full of water-based floor paints advertised as tough as industrial systems — most of that is exaggeration, and we don't want to be in that group. This is a good product on its own ground, and that ground has clear limits. Read these five points before you transfer.

It's a home and light-to-medium duty floor paint — not a factory floor

Home floors, private garages, balconies, walkways, shops, offices — this is where it wins · But floors with regular forklift traffic, dragged machinery or industrial chemical spills need the Epoxy-PU floor coating system outright. Don't force this one onto that job — you'll both be disappointed.

Dries fast, but full strength takes 7 days

Water-based means it's touch-dry quickly, but the film keeps cross-linking for days before it reaches its rated durability — for the first 7 days don't drag furniture, don't park a car, don't scrub hard and don't leave water standing on it. Impatience here is the number-one cause of marks on a new floor.

The paint only holds as well as the floor beneath it

Paint over a floor whose surface is friable, dusty or has old paint peeling and the new paint comes off together with that loose layer, however good the paint · Rub the floor hard: if white powder comes off on your hand, skim it with SolidFloor first, and finish cracks and holes with PatchPro before anything else.

New concrete must cure first

Freshly poured concrete needs at least 4 weeks before painting — uncured concrete is both damp and strongly alkaline, and even though our film resists alkali, a slab that hasn't settled is not worth the gamble. Wait it out, then paint.

The finish is smooth — wet floors still need care

The dry film gives an even, smooth surface that wipes clean — but a smooth surface plus water is always slippery. Zones that are regularly wet, like laundry yards or rain-exposed walkways, need the same care as any smooth floor. If slip resistance is critical for your job, message us to talk it through before ordering.

How to use — everything on this page

No manual to open anywhere else; all the real numbers are here — this job really is easy, but the prep step still decides how many years it lasts.

  1. Check the floor first — new concrete must be cured

    New concrete or render must cure at least 4 weeks before painting · On an old floor, rub hard with your palm: white powder on your hand means the surface is friable and must be fixed first with SolidFloor — painting over a friable surface is paying twice. New floor: wait 4 weeks

  2. Clean the floor and let it dry

    Sweep off dust and wash away oil, grease and algae completely — oil is the number-one enemy of adhesion. Wash with concentrated dish soap and rinse thoroughly · Then let the floor dry before starting. No oil or greaseFloor dry before painting

  3. Previously painted floor — sand lightly first

    Old paint that is still firmly bonded: sand until the surface is dull, then wipe off the dust, to give the new paint something to grip · Old paint that is blistered or peeling must be scraped off down to the floor — painting over peeling paint is trusting your floor to a layer that's already on its way out.

  4. Always stir before painting

    Open the tin and stir the whole tin through every time, especially one that has stood a while — the paint at the bottom is denser than the top. Stir with a stick to the bottom for about a minute; no drill mixer needed. Single component, nothing to add

  5. First coat with a roller — thin and even

    Use a short-nap roller and work in one direction across the floor. A thin, even film beats thick patches — water-based paint applied too thick dries slowly and risks roller marks · Start at the far corner and work back towards the door. Short-nap rollerApply thin

  6. Let it dry, then apply the second coat

    Touch-dry in about 30–60 minutes (no transfer on a light touch) · Recoat 2–4 hours after the first coat, applying the second coat across the direction of the first for the most even film — two coats is the standard for this job; one coat gives you colour but not durability. Touch-dry 30–60 minRecoat 2–4 hAlways 2 coats

  7. Waiting times — the full set of real numbers

    Light foot traffic after 24 hours · Furniture back and normal use after 3 days · Full strength, standing water and scrubbing after 7 days

    For the first 7 days avoid dragging heavy items, parking a car, hard scrubbing and standing water. Walk on it 24 hNormal use 3 daysFull strength 7 days

  8. Wash tools in plain water immediately

    It's water-based: wash the roller and tray in plain water before the paint dries, no thinner needed — let it dry on the roller and the tough polyurethane film we advertise will be tough on your roller instead.

The mistakes that waste it most often — never paint a wet floor or one still holding a lot of moisture / never paint outdoors if rain is due within 6 hours — water-based paint hit by rain before it dries is a washed-out job / never paint over a friable surface or peeling old paint / never thin with water to stretch it — a thinner film means the durability goes with it.
Storage — lid tight, in the shade; never leave the tin in the sun or where heat builds up, and never let it freeze — water-based paint that has frozen once cannot be recovered.
