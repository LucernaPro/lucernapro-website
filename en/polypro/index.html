500MICRON · TWO COATS

PolyPro is the highest-grade waterproofing in our lineup — Polyaspartic technology, the class the industry counts as Polyurea Gen3, at ≥95% solids, built for coating's harshest arenas: outdoors, hard sun, permanent immersion. The smooth semi-gloss film cleans easily — ideal for pools, tanks and rooftops.

Two coats build a dry film of 500–600 microns, with ≥100% elongation, ≥15 MPa tensile strength, and ≥4 MPa adhesion to concrete. What does that number mean? It means stronger than the concrete itself can bear — in pull-off tests the concrete usually fails before the film lets go. That same grip is what lets it bond even to glossy glazed tiles without a primer. And with ≥95% solids, it's nearly all product in the can, barely any solvent to evaporate away — that's why it costs more, and why it's worth more on jobs that can't fail.

How to use

6 steps, done — the heart of this job is weighing and stirring. Don't cut corners there, and the film will repay you for years.

  1. Prep the surface

    Wash off salt, grease and dirt completely; grind and clean concrete surfaces, then let everything dry. For regular jobs you can paint directly — no primer needed, PolyPro's own adhesion is high enough.

  2. Fill grout lines and cracks

    Tile work: chipped or missing grout must be fully repaired first — always. Concrete: patch cracks smooth. Skip this step and the waterproofing simply won't work.

  3. Weigh and mix — the make-or-break step

    Weigh Part A : Part B at 2 : 1 by weight on an actual scale — never eyeball it — then stir until fully uniform through the whole bucket. A wrong ratio or lazy stirring means the film never cures properly. And this one sets fast: once mixed you have about 10 minutes, so mix only as much as you can apply.

    A : B = 2 : 1 by weightWeigh on a scaleUse within ~10 minutes

  4. First coat

    Brush, roller or spray — cover every corner and joint. An even film beats thick patches every time.

  5. Second coat

    Once the first coat is dry enough to recoat, apply the second for the thickness the system needs.

    Wait ~6–8 hours

  6. Let it fully cure before filling

    Allow the film to cure completely before adding water or putting it to work — for immersion jobs, a little patience here buys a film that stays for the long haul.

⚠ Caution: Not suitable for people allergic to thinner or chemical products — if you have such allergies, have a contractor apply it. Always work in a well-ventilated space. Contains combustible ingredients: keep away from sparks and no smoking in the work area.
About slip resistance — straight talk: PolyPro's film is a smooth, handsome semi-gloss designed for pool walls, pool floors and immersion work — it is not designed as an anti-slip walking surface. If your job is a walkway where slipping matters, we recommend TileCoat Polyurea with anti-slip grit in the paint.
How to make the expensive layer last — our advice: PolyPro should always be the layer touching the substrate, because its adhesion is unreal. Then top it with a budget waterproofing as a sacrificial shield against sun and wear (we recommend SiliconePro) — come maintenance time, you repaint only the cheap top layer while the expensive one stays put. Visibly cheaper in the long run.
