Revival Rust Converter
It doesn't hide rust. It changes it.

How it actually works

The odd thing about this product compared with an anti-rust paint: it needs the rust. No rust, nothing to convert.

01

It converts the rust, it doesn't cover it

The product carries a mildly acidic system that reacts directly with the iron oxide, turning rust that is still chemically active into a stable compound that can't carry on spreading. The polymer in the formula then films over the top and shuts out moisture and oxygen. What you see with your own eyes is the orange-brown going dark — that colour change is the reaction happening.

02

Primer built in — one step out of the process

Once dry, the film is your primer — the finish paint goes straight over it, no separate primer to buy or apply. The usual job is sand the rust → prime → paint, three steps. This collapses it to Revival → paint. / To be clear though: it's a primer, not a finish — you still have to paint over it. The straight talk section below explains why.

03

Waterborne, low odour, very low VOC

This is a waterborne system, not a solvent one — under 1% VOC by weight, low odour, far more civilised to work with in an enclosed space, and no lead and no heavy metals in the formula. / Wash your tools in plain water while it's still wet. No thinner required.

04

Made for the jobs you can't sand

Structural steel, plant pipework, columns, tanks, fences, gates, chassis, vehicle underbodies — work where you'd be sanding tens or hundreds of square metres, or where the tight corners, deep channels and bolt heads are places a wire brush and a grinder simply cannot reach. That's where this earns its money.

Industrial grade — why contractors pick this one

Any working contractor knows de-rusting on site is nobody's idea of a good day — and the tighter the corner, the more hours and labour cost it eats. Then, if the preparation wasn't good enough, the paint goes on and fails from underneath a few months later anyway.

Take off only what's loose. The rust that is still firmly attached stays where it is and lets Revival do the work — the bigger the structure and the tighter the access, the more you gain. The places tools can't reach are exactly the places a liquid can.

It removes the de-rusting step and the priming step at the same time, leaving Revival → paint. / On a job costed in days of labour, the days you don't spend are the part that never shows up on the invoice for the product.

Primer in one

The reaction turns the existing rust into a stable film anchored to the steel that also does the priming job. Apply the finish paint directly once it's fully dry — and do apply it, because this isn't where the job ends.

STRAIGHT TALK — READ THIS BEFORE YOU PAY

It really does take a lot of grinding out of the job
but there are 5 things we'll tell you first

1 steprust converter + primer in a single product

One — it's a primer, not a finish. You always paint over it. First line, because people get this wrong: Revival is not designed to stand out in the sun and rain on its own. Its job is to stop the rust that's already there and leave a surface ready to take paint. / Leave it bare and it doesn't protect you well, and the rust will find its way back to you. Put the finish coat on and be done with it — the paint is the layer that takes the weather.

Two — never put it on painted parts. There's a mild acid in it. It was made to have a conversation with rust, not with paint — go over existing paint and you'll ruin the paint and get nothing you wanted, because there's no rust there for it to react with. Apply it to rusted or bare steel only, mask the edges where it meets sound paintwork, and if it splashes, wipe it off immediately while it's still wet.

Three — really heavy rust still has to be knocked back first. The whole mechanism depends on the liquid getting down into the rust and reaching all of it. If the rust has blistered into plates, is flaking loose, or has built into a thick crust, the liquid won't reach the bottom layer and that layer will carry on rusting under a surface that has apparently changed colour. Knock off and brush away everything that's loose, leave the rust that's still gripping the steel, then apply. / This is not a limitation of our brand. It's a limitation of every rust converter on earth.

Four — it may not go jet black, and that's fine. Different rust — different age, different exposure — gives a different result. Some areas go black, some dark brown, some grey, and one plate often isn't even across itself. / The test is "clearly darker than it was", not "uniformly black". If anywhere is still bright orange, the liquid hasn't reached it. Note it and go back over it.

Five — for small jobs it may not be worth buying. Plainly: a small part you can sand in ten minutes doesn't need a chemical. This pays off when the area is large or the shape genuinely can't be sanded — though if you just can't face the sanding, it'll do that job too. We won't judge.

"Paints you can apply straight over rust" — true or not?

Straight answer: products that genuinely do this exist. But almost every one of them carries the same line of small print — the rust must be tightly adhered and the loose scale has to come off first. Nothing on earth pours onto blistered, flaking rust and calls it finished. Anyone telling you otherwise is selling hope. / Revival plays by the same rule. The difference is that it doesn't merely cover the rust, it goes in and changes it.

Apply it wet — don't spread it thin like paint

This isn't paint, so don't feather it out for a nice even look — put enough on that it can actually soak into the rust, especially in channels, bolt heads and tight corners. / On heavy rust, go over it again once the first coat has dried. The aim is to reach the bottom of the rust, not to look tidy.

You must topcoat — and it must be properly dry first

The film has already done the priming for you, so there's no second primer — but there does have to be a finish coat. Don't stop at Revival. / And because it's waterborne, the water has to evaporate away completely before you seal it in with paint. Paint over a film that isn't fully dry and you trap moisture underneath — the problem comes back within months.

Tools wash out in water

The upside of waterborne: brushes, rollers and trays rinse out in plain water while still wet. No thinner. / Let it dry in a brush, though, and it's a genuine nuisance to get out — wash them the moment you stop.

Storage

Waterborne, so — seal the lid properly, keep it in the shade, out of direct sun, and don't let it freeze. Stir it back together before every use.

Safety

There's a mild acid in it and it is not flammable (waterborne), but treat it as an irritant — wear rubber gloves and eye protection, keep it out of your eyes, and wash skin with clean water straight away if it makes contact. / Low odour and low VOC, but still work with ventilation, and keep it away from children.

How to use it

Every number you need is on this page — and there are really only two things that matter: get all the loose material off, and put enough on that the liquid can get down to the rust.

  1. Check it's steel, then knock off everything loose

    Rusted or bare ferrous metal only — never galvanised, zinc-coated, aluminium or stainless (the acid strips the sacrificial zinc that is currently protecting the metal). / Then take off what's loose: blistered plates, flaking scale, powdery rust that falls away when you brush it. Hammer, wire brush or a power tool is fine. You do not need to go back to bright metal — rust that is still gripping the steel is what Revival is here to convert.

    Ferrous metal onlyOnly remove what's looseTight rust stays

  2. Get the surface clean, dry and free of oil

    Dust, dirt, grease and engine oil are all barriers between the liquid and the rust — degrease first, always, then let the surface dry completely before you start (a wet surface simply dilutes the product where it lands).

    Surface must be dryNo oil or grease

  3. Mask anything painted

    The hard rule for this product — keep it off existing paint. Run masking tape along the joins, or sheet off whatever isn't part of the job. / If it splashes, wipe it off straight away with a damp cloth while it's still wet. Don't let it dry there.

    Always mask paintworkSplash = wipe now

  4. Stir it back together

    Ready to use — nothing to mix, nothing to dilute — but stir or shake it properly every time, because it can separate on standing. / Don't add water to make it go further. Dilute it and you dilute the reaction with it.

    Ready to useNever add water

  5. First coat — apply it wet, brush or roller

    Apply it generously, not spread thin — the point is to get the liquid down into the rust, not to leave a smooth finish. / Work the brush hard into channels, bolt heads, weld seams and tight corners, because that is where the rust is deepest and why you bought this in the first place.

    Wet, not thinWork the corners

  6. Let it react — watch the colour

    Within about 15–30 minutes the rusted surface starts to go dark. / Again: it doesn't have to be jet black and it doesn't have to match across the piece — different rust, different result. Clearly darker = working. / Anywhere still bright orange hasn't been reached. Remember it and go back over it.

    Colour shifts in 15–30 minDarker = working

  7. Heavy rust — go over it a second time

    Wait until the first coat is dry to the touch, roughly 2–4 hours, then apply again, concentrating on anywhere the colour change was weak. / Light rust is a one-coat job. Heavy rust, or old steel that has been outside for years, is a two-coat job — allow for that when you order.

    2–4 hours between coatsHeavy rust = 2 coats

  8. Let it dry properly before the finish paint

    It's waterborne, so the water has to be gone before you seal it in — allow at least 24 hours, and stretch that to 48 hours in humid weather or on an important job. / Then paint straight over it — no separate primer needed. The film has already done that part.

    24 hours minimumNo second primer

  9. Wash your tools in water, immediately

    Brushes, rollers, trays — plain water while the product is still wet. No thinner needed. / Let it dry on them and it becomes very hard to remove.

    WaterDo it straight away
