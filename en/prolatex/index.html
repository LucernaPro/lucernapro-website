4 things you should know first

30 minis when clamps come off — not when it's fully cured

One — rain yes, submersion no. Let's be precise, because this is where people get it wrong most often. ProLatex is built for rain that lands and runs off. Normal outdoor exposure is comfortable territory. But if your joint is going to sit permanently underwater — aquariums, ponds, boat hulls, anything that stays submerged — this is the wrong product. Message us instead and we'll point you at the right one.

Two — 30 minutes is set time, not full strength. The 30-minute figure we advertise is set time: the clamps come off, the assembly holds its shape, you can move it. It is not yet ready to carry real load or go out into the weather. Give it more time first (see step 6 in the instructions). Rushing this point is the single most common reason a joint fails when the glue itself was never at fault.

Three — water-based means the surface must be dry. The upside of a water-based formula: no harsh solvent smell, and hands and tools wash off with plain water while it's still wet. The trade-off is a condition you have to respect — the surface must be genuinely dry, genuinely clean, free of oil and dust. Glue onto damp timber and you lose a large share of your bond strength without ever seeing it happen.

Four — the small tub costs nearly double per kilo. We're not hiding this number: 200 g works out to 550 THB/kg, while the 5 kg pail comes to 286 THB/kg. If you use this regularly or you're building for customers, repeatedly buying the little tub means paying close to twice as much for exactly the same glue. How to use it

Every number you need is on this page — and there aren't many, because this one is easy. Open the tub and go. There are exactly two ways to get it wrong: a surface that isn't dry, and going outside too soon.

  1. Check the surface really is dry and clean

    This is a water-based adhesive, so the surface must be dry, clean, dust-free and free of oil or wax. Freshly cut timber — brush off all the sawdust first. Timber that's been out in the rain — wait until it's properly dry. Don't rush this one.

    Dry · clean · oil-free

  2. Glossy surfaces → scuff them first

    Ordinary timber takes glue straight from the tub. But anything smooth and glossy — PVC, plastics, glossy foam, glazed tile, or timber that's already been lacquered — needs to be scuffed with 120–180 grit abrasive until the sheen is gone, then dusted off. Every adhesive on earth grips a matte surface better than a shiny one.

    120–180 grit until matte

  3. Thin coat, full coverage

    A small amount does the job. Covering the whole mating face matters far more than laying it on thick — excess glue wastes product, squeezes out along the edges and slows drying. For softwoods and absorbent surfaces (plywood, end grain, cut edges) we recommend coating both faces, since the timber will draw some of the adhesive into itself.

    Thin but completeAbsorbent surfaces = both faces

  4. Bring the parts together within 5–10 minutes

    Once it's on, don't leave it sitting — bring the two faces together within roughly 5–10 minutes, while the adhesive is still wet. If you wait until the surface starts to skin over, you won't get full bond strength. Press the joint firmly closed so no air is trapped between the parts.

    Close the joint in 5–10 minPress out trapped air

  5. Clamp or weight it for 30 minutes

    Here's the number that sells this product — 30 minutes under pressure and the clamps come off. No overnight wait. No clamps? Weights work, so do rope or rubber straps — all that matters is even pressure across the whole joint. And wipe the squeeze-out with a damp cloth right now, while it's still wet.

    30 min clamp = setWipe squeeze-out while wet

  6. Give it 24 hours before load or weather

    Clamps off means you can handle the piece — it does not mean full strength yet. Allow roughly 24 hours before putting real load on the joint or moving the piece outdoors into the rain. Finishing a build in the evening and leaving it out in that night's downpour is the classic failure, and the glue isn't what went wrong.

    24 h before full load24 h before rain exposure

  7. Wash tools with plain water

    The upside of water-based — brushes, spreaders and hands wash off with plain water while the adhesive is still wet. No solvent needed. Let it dry on your tools and you're down to scraping or sanding, so clean up straight after every session.

    Plain water, no solvent

  8. Storage — seal it properly

    Leftover product: seal the lid tightly and store somewhere shaded, cool and dry, out of direct sun. Wipe the rim clean before you close it — dried adhesive around the rim is why the lid won't come off next time, and why air gets in and spoils what's left.

    Tight lid · shade · dry
⚠ Not for permanently submerged joints: ProLatex handles rain that lands and runs off, but it is not designed to sit underwater. Aquariums, ponds, boat hulls, or any joint that stays submerged — don't use this one. Message us first and we'll recommend the right product.
⚠ Basic precautions: wear gloves while working and avoid eye contact. If it gets in the eyes, rinse immediately with plenty of clean water and seek medical attention. Keep out of reach of children and don't use it for anything other than its intended purpose.
Working conditions: work somewhere with normal air movement, and avoid gluing while it's actually raining or when humidity is extremely high — a water-based adhesive has to release its water in order to set. On very humid days, allow more than the stated 30 minutes.
