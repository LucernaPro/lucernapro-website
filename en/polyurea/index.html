How to use

6 steps, all on this page — the heart of this product is the weighing and the stirring. Don't cut corners on those two, and everything else is easy.

  1. Prep the surface

    Clean until bone dry — no dust, no grease, no grime. Surfaces previously painted, waxed, or primed must be scraped completely bare; it cannot go over old coatings. Then tape off the edges of your work area.

  2. Fill every crack first — always

    Cracks, holes, and grooves in the cement must be repaired flush before coating — a waterproofing system has to be one continuous skin. If the surface doesn't bond into one piece, it won't waterproof, and the whole system loses strength.

  3. Weigh and mix — the make-or-break step

    Weigh Part A : Part B at 2 : 1 by weight on a scale, and only a scale — never eyeball it. Then stir a full 1 minute until truly uniform, all the way to the bottom of the bucket — a lazy stir means the coating never fully cures and the job fails later. Once mixed you have 10 minutes to apply, so mix small batches you can finish in time.

    A : B = 2 : 1 by weightScale only — no eyeballingStir 1 full minute, to the bottomUse within 10 minutes

  4. First coat — as thin as possible

    Use a short-nap 4-inch roller (a brush works on small areas but leaves streaks). On the first coat, press the roller hard and spread it as thin as you can — this coat has one job only: stopping the surface from drinking too much of your material. Looks don't matter yet.

  5. Second coat — build the thickness

    Wait 2 hours after the first coat, fit a fresh roller sleeve (and again whenever the sleeve starts to stiffen), then finish the job: even color across the whole surface and full thickness for strength.

    2 hours between coatsNew roller sleeve each coat

  6. Wait 6 hours, then it's back in service

    After the final coat, leave it 6 hours and the floor is ready for normal use — for upkeep, plain water or dish soap is all it takes, and it scrubs fine.

    Ready for use in 6 hours
⚠ Safety notes: This is a solvent-base formula — always work with good ventilation. Contents are flammable: no sparks, no smoking anywhere near the work area. Not suitable for anyone with thinner or chemical sensitivities — if that's you, don't apply it yourself; have a contractor do it.
Tile jobs — don't gamble with this one: This product was born for bare concrete. If your surface is glazed tile, go straight to PolyPro (pools / permanent immersion) or TileCoat Polyurea (walkable floors with anti-slip grit) instead.
About slip resistance — straight talk: The cured film is smooth, with no anti-slip grit in it. It is not designed as an anti-slip walking floor — if slip matters on your floor, look at TileCoat Polyurea.
