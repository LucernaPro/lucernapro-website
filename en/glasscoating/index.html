How glass can clean itself

It comes down to one word — superhydrophilic. A surface that loves water so much that droplets simply cannot form on it.

01

Water sheets off, never beads

With a water contact angle as low as ≤ 7°, rain spreads into a thin film and runs off, flushing loose dust and grime down with it — every rainfall is a free wash.

02

Truly invisible — when applied thin

92.7% light transmittance and a film far too thin to see — but apply it too thick and iridescent rainbow marks appear. Read Straight Talk #1 before deciding.

03

Inorganic film, 6H hard

The cured film is inorganic, so by nature it does not yellow or degrade under sunlight the way organic films do. 6H pencil hardness with Grade 0 cross-cut adhesion — the best rating on the scale.

04

Fewer rope-access cleaning cycles

On high-rise buildings, every facade-cleaning cycle costs real money. The technology developer’s data puts the reduction at roughly 3 out of 4 manual cleaning cycles per year — that is where the coating pays for itself.

The principle

A superhydrophilic surface works the opposite way to car ceramic coatings — instead of making water bead and roll, it makes water flatten into a thin sheet and drain off the whole pane.

What you get

No lingering droplets means no water spots, and loosely attached dust gets carried down by the water film every time it rains.

The bonus most people miss

The film is also anti-static — dry dust has a harder time sticking in the first place, so you are not only relying on rain.

The second mechanism — photocatalytic

Sunlight activates the layer to continuously break down organic grime on the surface (nano-photocatalysis) — sun works by day, rain does the flushing. Two mechanisms working together.

Project jobs — our standard 6-step process

For curtain walls and whole-building work, every project follows the same sequence.

  1. Site survey & feasibility check

    We assess the glass type, installation condition and site constraints, then plan the work and the test protocol.

  2. Small-area test before committing

    A test patch on the project’s own glass confirms adhesion and self-cleaning performance before full-scale application.

  3. Deep cleaning

    Neutral cleaner and soft tools remove dust, oil and water stains, leaving a residue-free surface.

  4. Coating application

    Sprayed onto completely dry glass in a thin, even layer — with no change to the glass’s appearance or clarity.

  5. Natural curing

    The film cures at ambient temperature into a dense layer bonded to the glass surface.

  6. Inspection & handover

    We check the self-cleaning result and visual consistency across every pane before signing off.

How to apply — everything on this page

All the real numbers are right here — read to the end before opening the bottle, especially the rule about water before full cure.

  1. Get the glass clean and completely dry

    Remove all dust, oil and old residue. Any grease must be degreased off first — surface cleanliness decides the adhesion of the entire job.

  2. Spray or wipe on a thin, even layer — thin is everything

    For large areas use an HVLP spray gun, 1.0–1.2 mm tip at 0.2 MPa (about 2 bar). For small jobs, apply by hand with a fine-pore sponge (melamine sponge) or non-woven cloth. Never apply a thick coat — excess thickness is the cause of rainbow marks and wastes product with no added benefit.

    HVLP 1.0–1.2 mm0.2 MPa15–50 ml/m²

  3. Surface dries in 5 minutes

    The surface sets very quickly after application — do not go back and re-wipe an area once it has started to set, or you will mark the film.

    Surface dry 5 min

  4. Keep water off until the film sets

    The most critical rule of this job — water touching the surface before full set leaves permanent watermarks. Initial set takes about 1 hour. For outdoor work, always check the rain forecast before starting.

    Initial set ~1 hr

  5. Let it cure fully, and the job is done

    Full cure at room temperature takes about 6 hours in hot weather, up to 20 hours in cool weather. Factory jobs that need speed can heat-cure at 70°C in about 1 minute.

STRAIGHT TALK — WHAT MOST SELLERS WON'T SAY

Read these 7 points before paying —
this is not a magic product, and we'd rather you know exactly what it is

7THINGS TO KNOW BEFORE BUYING
1 · Thick coats show rainbow marks — if you can't accept that, don't buy this

Apply the film too thick and you get iridescent rainbow marks (thin-film interference) — the same optical phenomenon as multi-coated camera lenses. It is not dirt and cannot be wiped off, appearing as a rainbow sheen in reflected light, and on dark or tinted glass it is even more visible. Correct thin application reduces it greatly, but we will not guarantee you will never see it — if that is a dealbreaker for you, do not buy this product. If you can accept it, what you get in return is a massive reduction in glass-cleaning work.

2 · This is not a car ceramic coating

Automotive coatings are hydrophobic — water beads and rolls away. This product is the exact opposite: water flattens into a sheet. If you coat your car with this expecting beading, you will be disappointed. For cars, see Schutz Nano 9H.

3 · Water is still the carrier

Sunlight breaks down organic grime (photocatalysis), but what actually carries dirt off the glass is the water film. Indoor glass that never sees rain mainly gets the anti-static dust reduction, and bonded stains like spray paint or tree sap will not come off on their own — they still need wiping (though it becomes easier).

4 · The curing window is the danger zone

Water on the surface before the film sets = permanent watermarks that can only be fixed by recoating. Check the rain forecast before any outdoor job — this is the number-one cause of failed applications for this product category.

5 · Some surfaces cannot be coated

Materials that repel alcohol (such as surfaces previously treated with certain stain repellents) will not accept the coating — always test a small corner before doing the whole pane. Plasma/corona-treated surfaces may work; ask us first.

6 · Wrong maintenance can ruin the film

After coating, clean only with a soft, water-dampened cloth. No hard brushes or abrasive scrubbing, and absolutely no alkaline cleaners of pH 11 or higher. Mild or neutral glass cleaners are fine.

7 · Not a coat-once-forever product

The high-efficiency period outdoors is around 5 years (the film slowly wears from natural wind and dust), after which performance declines gradually — it does not vanish overnight. Recoating the same glass restores it.
