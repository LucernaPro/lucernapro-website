460% ELONGATION AT BREAK

A shoe flexes thousands of times a day. A rock-hard glue cracks and peels within weeks — FlexGrip takes the opposite path: a HydroCure Polymer that stretches to 460% while still holding 33 MPa of tensile strength. It follows the flex all the way, and the grip never leaves. That's why it can replace stitching, while ordinary glues can only "hold on for now".

The glue itself is a clear, easy-flowing liquid that wicks into material grooves — and it's built to live inside the joint between two parts, not to sit exposed in the sun: any exposed bead may naturally yellow over time under UV. The grip doesn't fade, but clean work means a thin coat, a tight joint, and no squeeze-out left for the sun to find.

Its arena

Detached, peeling, delaminating soles — plus every household repair: loose tiles, cracked fan blades, broken bricks, a cracked battery case, a guitar neck. Our customers take it further than we imagined, every year.

Its temperament

Fresh out of the bottle it looks thin as water — don't panic. It's very much a real glue, just deliberately slow. Strap it for 12 hours before you judge it.

The one rule you can't break

Never apply it thick and oozing — if it squeezes out, wipe it immediately while still wet and it comes off easily. Let it dry and it's there forever. Real power glue can't be undone. Thin and even is the only way.

STRESS–STRAIN CURVE · Adhesive film tested to JIS K 6251
The harder you stretch it, the harder it fights back

The film doesn't just stretch to 4.6× its length — the further it stretches, the steeper the curve climbs, breaking only at 33 MPa ≈ roughly 330 kg hanging from every square centimeter of glue. That's high-grade elastomer behavior: rubber-tough, rope-hard to break — and exactly why it goes with the flex of a sole on every step.

How to use

Five steps, everything on this page — the heart of it: "strap it tight, then be patient." This glue rewards people who can wait.

  1. Clean the surface, let it dry completely

    Wipe away dust, dirt and grease — no sanding, no primer needed. But never apply onto a wet surface: it needlessly cuts the glue's performance.

  2. Apply a thin coat — one side or both

    Cover the full contact area; one side works, both sides work. The iron rule: never apply thick and oozing — if any squeezes out, wipe it immediately while wet; once dry it won't come off. / Pro tip for wide gaps (like a loose tile): lay glue-soaked tissue paper into the gap to expand the bonding area — saves glue and grips harder.

    Thin coat = divineSqueeze-out: wipe while wet

  3. Press together, strap it tight

    Rubber bands, tape, clamps or a heavy weight — whatever's in the house, as long as the parts stay pressed together the whole time. You can still reposition calmly during this stage. / For shoes — this matters: stuff the shoe full with cloth or paper to hold its shape before strapping or taping. If it dries deformed, real power glue can't be undone — a deformed shoe is a lost shoe. Don't gamble here.

    Rubber bands / clamps / tapeShoes: stuff to shape first, always

  4. Wait 12 hours before unstrapping

    The surface starts setting around 6 hours, but keep it strapped a full 12 hours before removing anything — unstrapping early is the number-one cause of failed repairs.

    Surface set ~6 hStrapped 12 h

  5. Heavy duty? Wait for full cure

    Normal walking is fine after unstrapping, but before hard use or water, give it around 40 hours to reach full cure — one more patient day buys you a bond that lasts for years.

    Full cure ~40 h

⚠ Safety first: Work in a ventilated area, wear gloves, keep the glue off skin and eyes — if it gets in your eyes, rinse with plenty of water and see a doctor. Keep out of reach of children. / Glue on your hands? Best method: pour dry detergent powder onto your hands and scrub without water until the tackiness is gone, then rinse (dish soap with repeated scrubbing is the backup). / Never use thinner or strong acids on your skin — dangerous, and this glue laughs at thinner anyway. It won't come off.
Storage — this glue's enemy is moisture: FlexGrip cures by reacting with moisture in the air, so humid air sneaking into the bottle is what kills it early. After use, wipe the bottle mouth and cap clean (or next time the cap won't open), seal the mouth with tape as an extra barrier, then close tightly. Store somewhere dry, out of sunlight, at normal room temperature — never refrigerate: fridge humidity and the condensation that forms when you take it in and out are prime glue killers. Standard shelf life: 6 months.
