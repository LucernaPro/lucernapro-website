How to use

Everything you need is on this page — epoxy grouting comes down to two things: scrape the old grout out completely and stir the mix until truly uniform. Nail those two and the rest is easy.

  1. Scrape out all the old grout

    Use a grout-removal tool to dig the old cement grout out down to the joint, then brush and wipe the channel clean and dry — new grout bonds only as well as the joint beneath it.

  2. Tape both sides of every joint

    Run masking tape parallel to the joint on both sides — the iron rule from real jobsites: no tape, no grouting. Epoxy that cures on a tile face is brutally hard to remove.

    No tape = no start

  3. Mix Part A and Part B at 1:1

    Measure A and B in equal parts, 1:1, then stir until truly uniform — one even colour, no streaks. Mix one small batch at a time; never the whole jar at once, or it sets in the cup.

    A:B = 1:1Stir to one colourSmall batches

  4. Work the grout into the joint

    Press the mix into the joint between the tape lines, packing it full with no voids — an air pocket today is a water channel tomorrow.

  5. Tool the line smooth

    Use a popsicle stick or any smooth-edged tool to press the grout down and strike the line flat and even from end to end.

  6. Peel the tape off immediately

    Finish a line, peel its tape right away while the grout is still fresh — wait until it hardens and the edge tears ragged.

    Peel while fresh

  7. Wait 4–6 hours — done

    Let it set for 4–6 hours and the area is back in service — keep it dry while it cures.

    Set time 4–6 hrs
⚠ Don't forget: mixed epoxy has a limited working time before it starts to set — plan the job zone by zone, mix only what you can place in time, and wipe any smears off tile faces immediately while still fresh.
