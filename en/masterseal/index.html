Master Seal the waterproofer
that lives inside the mortar

Almost every waterproofer on the market is something you paint onto the face of the mortar. This one isn't. Master Seal is a hydrophobic powder admix that goes in at the mixer — and that bag of cement comes out water-repellent all the way through its body. Water beads on the surface instead of being wicked into the pores like a drinking straw. The result: the surface stops holding water, it dries out faster, and dark staining and algae have a far harder time getting a grip. Works in renders, masonry mortar, concrete and tile grout — 50 kg of cement needs just 100 g. From 230 THB.

Why it belongs in the mix, not on the surface

Because a surface coat is the layer that wears, peels and gets scrubbed off — whereas what sits inside the mortar stays as long as the mortar does

01

It stops water at the cause: capillary absorption

Ordinary mortar contains millions of tiny pores that behave like drinking straws, pulling water in on their own with no pressure required. Master Seal makes the walls of those pores water-repellent, so the water is never drawn in — a marked drop in how much water the material holds. And here's the part that separates it from a film-forming sealer: the pores stay open, so the wall still breathes. Moisture already inside can get out instead of being trapped until it lifts your paint.

02

Putting it in tile grout is the trick customers love most

Got a tiler who flatly refuses to use proper premium grout and insists on the bag of cement grout he's always used? Don't fight him. Put 2% of this in that bag instead. The joints stop drinking water, dark staining and mould get a much harder grip, and the joints don't crumble as fast. It's not the equal of real premium grout — but compared with nothing at all, it isn't even close.

03

It doesn't interfere with the mortar

This is why it's used in bagged dry-mix plants: no retardation, no foaming, and no loss of compressive strength, adhesion or surface hardness. More important to the man holding the trowel: workability doesn't change, even at elevated dosage — plenty of admixtures start making the mortar feel strange once you add more. This one doesn't.

04

Concentrated. You use almost nothing

Standard dosage is just 0.2% by weight of binder — 50 kg of cement takes 100 g. An 800 g pack treats roughly 400 kg of cement. And the dosage response is linear, so if you want more repellency you simply add proportionally more. No guesswork.

0.2%by weight of binder — more isn't endlessly better

One — you get exactly one chance to add it, and that's at the mixer. This is not a liquid you paint on. Mortar that has already been laid and set cannot be treated by smearing powder over it; nothing happens. If your problem is a wall or floor that is already built and leaking right now, this is the wrong product — look at DeepSeal (negative-side waterproofing) or AquaShell for walls instead.

Two — overdose it and paint won't stick. Plainly: more product does mean more beading, but a mortar surface made aggressively water-repellent will not accept waterborne paint. If the surface is going to be painted, stay within 0.2–0.5%. You can chase maximum beading if you want, but accept that painting over it becomes difficult or impossible — and test a small patch every single time.

Three — cracked mortar still leaks. This product fixes how much water the body of the mortar absorbs. It does not fix cracks, and it is not a membrane that will stand up to continuous water pressure. Swimming pools, water tanks, ponds, roof decks — treat this as a reinforcing layer inside the mortar, never the finished answer. You still need a coating system on top (see the cards at the end of the page).

Four — mix it badly and you've wasted your money. Uneven distribution means part of the batch is water-repellent and part of it isn't, and water always finds the weakest point. The rule is absolute: blend the powder into the dry mix first, then add water — never sprinkle it into mortar that has already been wetted.

Dosage (by weight of dry binder)

Cement, render, masonry mortar, concrete: 0.2–0.5% — 50 kg cement = 100 g Master Seal
Tile jointing mortar (grout): 2% — 1 kg grout = 20 g Master Seal
The full ladder: general work starts at 0.2% → 0.5% for heavier water exposure or visibly stronger beading → up to 1% where a high degree of repellency is needed → beyond that you are in thin-section, permanently-wet territory like a grout joint at 2%.

Which cementitious systems it works in

Renders and plasters, masonry mortar, decorative renders, concrete, cementitious tile adhesive, tile jointing mortar and sealing mortars / slurries — if cement is the binder, this works. Only the dosage changes.

Less water in = the mortar lasts longer

The benefit people overlook: water is the carrier for nearly every problem inside mortar — the white salt bloom that surfaces (efflorescence), algae, mould, and the degradation of the mortar itself. Cut down what gets in and you cut several root causes at once.

Compatible with other admixtures

Use it alongside bonding agents, polymer powders, accelerators and retarders and the standard mortar additives — nothing fights. And it works normally in the high-alkaline environment (around pH 11–12) that cement produces anyway.

Store it properly or lose it

It's a free-flowing, non-dusting dry powder, but it takes up moisture — keep it in the original container, sealed, dry, below 25°C, and use it within 6 months. A pack left open or stored damp will cake, and caked powder will not distribute through a batch.

Where the money goes furthest

On a budget? Treat only the outermost render coat. That's the layer taking the rain directly, and it already does most of the work — there's no need to dose the entire structure.

How to use it

Every number you need is on this page — the steps are short, but the order matters more than people expect. Powder into the dry mix, always, before any water.

  1. Pick the dosage that matches the job

    Cement, render, masonry mortar, concrete → 0.2–0.5% (0.2% is plenty for general work; move to 0.5% for heavy water exposure or if you want visibly stronger beading)
    Tile jointing mortar → 2% — the rate is higher because a grout joint is a thin section that genuinely sits wet
    Every figure is a percentage of the dry binder weight, not the water.

    Mortar 0.2–0.5%Grout 2%

  2. Weigh it. Don't eyeball it

    This powder is concentrated, and the difference between 0.2% and 1% is the difference between "paints normally" and "paint won't stick". 50 kg cement at 0.2% = 100 g Master Seal, which is exactly one small pack. 1 kg grout at 2% = 20 g. A digital kitchen scale is accurate enough.

    50 kg cement = 100 g1 kg grout = 20 g

  3. Sprinkle it into the dry mix and blend it in first

    This is the step people get wrong most often — blend the powder through the dry mix until the colour is uniform with no white pockets sitting in clumps. Never sprinkle it into mortar that has already been mixed with water: it will ball up and fail to distribute, which leaves you with a batch that is water-repellent in some places and not in others.

    Powder into dry mix, alwaysBlend to uniform colour

  4. Add water as normal and mix

    Don't add or reduce water from your usual mix, and don't add anything else to "help" it. The best order is water in the vessel first, then the pre-blended powder into the water while stirring — tipping water onto a heap of dry powder gives you lumps that are miserable to break down. Mix until uniform, but don't run it hard and fast for a long time, because that entrains air into the mortar (and air voids in hardened mortar are the water paths of the future).

    Water first → powder inSame water as alwaysDon't whip air into it

  5. Let the mix stand 1–5 minutes, then stir briefly again

    Give the powder time to wet out fully before you start work — let it stand about 1–5 minutes, then stir gently once more. The mortar will feel smoother and work better than if you go straight in.

    Stand 1–5 min

  6. Render, lay, pour or grout exactly as you always do

    Nothing about the trade changes — working time is the same, there's no retardation, and the feel under the trowel is the same or marginally better. For grouting: press into the joints, tool the surface, wipe back the haze, all as usual.

    Same working time

  7. Cure normally — the beading appears as the surface dries

    Cure the mortar properly, the same as any other job (don't let it dry too fast and craze). The effect shows clearly once the surface is fully dry and the mortar has cured. Throw some water at it: it beads and rolls instead of vanishing into a dark patch.

    Visible once fully dry

  8. Painting over it? Test a small patch first. Every time.

    At 0.2–0.5% it paints normally — but our rule is always trial a small area first. Paint a palm-sized test patch, let it dry, then try to scrape it. If you've gone above 0.5%, plan on that surface not being painted from the outset.

    ≤0.5% = paintableTest patch, always

  9. Serious waterproofing still needs a coating on top

    Swimming pools, ponds, water tanks, roof decks, upstairs bathrooms — Master Seal inside the mortar is a reinforcing layer, not the finished answer. Fill any cracks and spalls completely first with FillerAce or DeepStick, then put the right coating system over the top for that job (see the cards at the end of the page).

    A reinforcing layer, not the answer
⚠ Don't dose it higher than you need: raise the rate and you do get more beading, but the risk that paint won't bond rises with it — if the surface is going to be painted, stay in the 0.2–0.5% band and test first.
⚠ Not a substitute for a waterproofing membrane: this reduces how much water the body of the mortar absorbs. It is not a waterproof sheet holding back standing pressure, and it does not fill cracks — pools, tanks, ponds and roof decks always need a coating layer on top.
⚠ Caked powder is compromised powder: powder that has taken up moisture won't distribute properly through a batch. Keep it sealed and dry, and use it within 6 months.
For plants pre-blending it into bagged product: Master Seal can be dry-blended, powder into powder, in any standard compulsory mixer — no special equipment needed. The one thing to watch is not letting the mixer temperature climb from shear forces. The blended product bags and stores normally and can be wetted out later. Basic safety: it's a dry, low-dusting, odourless powder — but this is still cement work. Wear gloves and a dust mask, as you should with cement anyway (cement itself is strongly alkaline and attacks skin). Avoid breathing dust and keep it out of your eyes. Keep out of reach of children.
