ROOT CAUSE — why scrubbing never wins

The stain that returns every year isn't poor scrubbing —
it's because the roots are still in there

2-IN-1CURATIVE · PREVENTIVE

Mould, algae and lichen on render, stone or tile don't just sit on top — they anchor themselves inside the pore structure. A pressure washer or stiff brush removes only what you can see. What's embedded stays alive, and the next damp spell brings it right back to the same spot.

And it's not just cosmetic — these organisms genuinely damage the surface: they undermine paint-film adhesion until the paint peels, and they hold permanent moisture against the substrate, accelerating decay of both the coating and the material underneath.

Paint peeling when the paint isn't the problem

Growth working its way under the film is a classic cause of paint peeling in sheets — repaint over it without treating first and it peels again in the same place

Trapped moisture = faster decay

A layer of algae and mould behaves like a sponge, holding moisture permanently against the surface — coatings and substrate degrade faster than they should

The correct fix

Use a wash that acts on the organisms in depth — curative on existing growth, and preventive as surface prep before repainting so it doesn't come back under the new film. That is exactly what BioShield is built for.

How to use

  1. Apply over the entire affected area

    Ready to use straight from the jug — apply by brush, roller or sprayer, wetting the surface thoroughly wherever mould, algae or lichen is present, plus a small margin past the visible edge (colonisation always extends further than the eye can see)

    1 litre ≈ 4–5 m²No dilution

  2. Leave it to act

    Don't rinse — let the wash work on the surface for about 1 hour so it can act on the growth and the organisms within it

    Leave ~1 hr

  3. Remove the residue

    When the time is up, remove loosened growth with a stiff brush or a pressure washer, as the surface allows — dead growth comes away far more easily than it did before treatment

    Stiff brush / pressure washer

  4. Maintain — and use it before repainting

    For maintenance, re-treat the moment staining or growth reappears; don't wait for it to spread. For surfaces about to be repainted, apply BioShield as a preventive treatment before coating, so mould and algae can't re-establish under the new paint film

    Repainting? BioShield first
⚠ Safety: Wear rubber gloves and eye protection; avoid splashes to eyes and skin — rinse with plenty of clean water on contact · Harmful to aquatic life — keep the product and rinse water out of fish ponds, aquaculture and drains that lead to natural watercourses · Never mix with other cleaners or chemicals · Keep out of reach of children and pets

5THINGS MOST SELLERS WON'T SAY
1 · This is not a stain bleach

BioShield deals with the organisms — dark staining that died and embedded years ago won't vanish in a blink like a bleach commercial. It needs the brush or pressure-washer step to come off. The trade-off: the growth is actually dead, not just faded and quietly growing back

2 · Permanently damp spots will recolonise

Prevention substantially extends the clean period, but a wall that stays wet and shaded year-round is algae's dream home — for a permanent fix, address the moisture source too (waterproofing, drainage). We can advise

3 · It's not a paint and not a waterproofer

This is a surface treatment — it forms no film and stops no water. Its job is to leave the surface clean and biologically sound, ready for painting or coating on top

4 · Never mix it with anything

Mixing with other cleaners can destroy its effectiveness or cause unwanted reactions — use it neat, on its own, exactly as the steps above describe

5 · Mind the fish pond

A wash that kills small organisms is, unsurprisingly, harmful to aquatic life — on sites with fish or shrimp ponds, or drains running to a canal, contain and direct the rinse water carefully
