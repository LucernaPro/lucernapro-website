Why HeatShield

This was never built to be the toughest waterproofer in our range. It was built to be the one that costs least per square metre — and it's the only one in the family that cools the roof as well.

01

Two jobs, one pass

Normally you buy one product to waterproof and a different one to reflect heat. This does both in the same coat: a flexible water-resistant film with a white reflective surface. The labour and the weekend you didn't spend twice is the part people forget to count.

Goes onto almost any roof

Metal sheet · tiled roofs · concrete decks · polycarbonate sheet — and its main job is old roofs: buying back several more years of service without tearing the whole thing off and starting again.

So how does it actually reflect heat?

No physics degree required. The hero of this story is the hollow ceramic microsphere, and there are millions of them packed into the dried film.

01

The problem: a dark roof is a heat battery

Sunlight landing on your roof is radiation. A dark surface, or tired old metal sheet, absorbs it and converts it into stored heat — which then radiates down into the room below all day while your air-conditioner fights a losing battle.

02

Line one: bounce it before it gets in

A matt white surface densely loaded with ceramic spheres behaves like millions of tiny mirrors — reflecting solar radiation straight back out instead of letting it soak into the roof. That's what the TSR above 95% figure measures.

03

Line two: the spheres are hollow, and that's the point

"Hollow" is doing real work here. Each sphere has air sealed inside it, and air is a terrible conductor of heat. Whatever radiation slips past the reflection stage now has to travel downward much more slowly.

04

Line three: let it go, don't hoard it

Emissivity 0.75 is the film's ability to radiate stored heat back out to the sky. A surface that reflects well but emits badly is still holding heat at sunset. This one lets it go — and the net result is a roof surface 5°C cooler or better.

5°C+THAT IS THE ROOF SURFACE, NOT THE ROOM

One — the 5°C is measured at the roof surface. It is not a promise that your living room drops exactly five degrees. How much you actually feel depends on your insulation, your ceiling and how well the roof space ventilates. A sealed, airless roof cavity will benefit less than a well-ventilated one. We sell coatings, not air-conditioning.

Two — this is the bottom rung of our waterproofing ladder. It genuinely stops rain, driven water and roof leaks. It is not the product for permanent standing water or immersion. A deck that ponds for weeks on end, a pond, a pool — that's not this can's job. Move up to SiliconePro or above.

Three — white is a function, not a styling choice. All of the reflective performance comes from a white, matt surface, which is why we sell it in white only — not because we can't make other colours, but because another colour throws away the entire point of the product. Paint over it in a dark shade later and you've binned the TSR you paid for, keeping only the waterproofing.

Four — dirt on white costs you reflectance. This is true of every cool-roof coating from every brand, not a flaw unique to ours. A white surface collecting dust, soot and algae for a year will reflect less than it did on day one. Rinsing it down periodically is maintenance you should plan for — do it and the performance comes back.

Five — it bridges about 2 mm, and no more. The film is elastic enough to span hairline crazing and small movement. Anything wider than that has to be filled and repaired first. Coating over a crack that is still open is just scheduling the same crack for later — the same house rule that applies to every coating we sell.

Six — bare, never-painted concrete needs a primer. That isn't an upsell. Raw concrete drinks the coating and the surface isn't smooth enough yet. Budget for HeatShield Primer from the start — the price is in the table below. Previously painted surfaces, metal sheet, tile and polycarbonate skip this step entirely.

How to apply it

Every number you need is on this page — no separate manual to hunt down. This is one of the easiest products we make: nothing to mix, no clock to beat. The surface preparation, though, is as strict as it is for everything else.

  1. Get the surface clean and dry

    The substrate must be free of dust, oil, grease and efflorescence. On a roof that has been painted before, strip off anything flaking, blistering or chalking down to a layer that is genuinely sound — coating over paint that is about to let go is just coating over the problem.

    Clean · dry · no loose paint

  2. Fill cracks and joints first

    This film bridges about 2 mm — anything wider gets repaired first, every time.
    Metal-sheet roofing: sheet laps, screw heads and ridge joints — seal them with SpackleFlex before you start.
    Concrete decks: cracks and spalls in the slab get filled flush with PatchPro, then left to dry as that page specifies — it's the repair product that pairs best with deck waterproofing.

    Bridges ~2 mmMetal sheet → SpackleFlexConcrete deck → PatchPro

  3. Bare, never-painted concrete gets primer

    Concrete or render that has never been painted takes one coat of HeatShield Primer to improve adhesion and even out the surface. Then wait roughly 30 minutes, or until it no longer feels tacky, before the first coat. Painted surfaces, metal sheet, tile and polycarbonate: skip this step.

    Primer = raw concrete onlyWait ~30 min / until not tacky

  4. First coat

    Brush, roller or airless spray — open the lid and go, nothing to mix, just stir it uniform first. Never add water to stretch it: a thinner film costs you the reflectance and the durability together.

    Brush · roller · airless spray1 kg ≈ 5 sq.m. (2 coats)No water

  5. Wait 1–2 hours

    The surface is touch dry within minutes (surface dry inside 15 minutes, hard dry inside 40), but give it 1–2 hours before recoating, adjusted for the weather — faster in bright sun and moving air, slower on a grey humid day.

    Surface dry < 15 minRecoat at 1–2 h

  6. Second coat — cross-wise to the first

    Lay the second coat across the direction of the first (first coat along the ribs, second across them) to cover brush lines and any thin spots. Two coats is the standard for this product, not an option — both the waterproofing and the reflectance depend on film thickness.

    Always 2 coatsSecond coat cross-wise

  7. In service after about 4 hours

    Allow roughly 4 hours after the final coat before calling it done. Until then keep water off it and stay off it — a film that is still wet is extremely slippery, which is not what you want underfoot on a roof.

    In service ~4 h

  8. Wash your tools out immediately, in water

    The upside of a waterborne product: brushes, rollers and buckets rinse out in plain water while the material is still wet, no thinner required. Let it dry in the bristles, though, and it becomes a genuine nuisance — so rinse the moment you put the tool down.

    Plain water · before it dries
⚠ Check the sky before you open the lid: don't apply if rain is a possibility within 4 hours · never onto standing water or a surface that is still wet · avoid applying below 10°C.
⚠ The real hazard on this job is the height, not the chemistry: this is waterborne, low odour, with no flash point detected — but you are working on a roof. Use a proper anchor point and have someone on the ground. Freshly coated surfaces are very slippery — do not walk on them. Plan your route out before you start so you never have to walk back across what you've just coated. Wear gloves and eye protection; if it splashes in your eyes, rinse with clean water.
Long-term care (the part sellers tend to leave out): a reflective surface works because it is white — so as dust, soot and algae build up, reflectance gradually falls. That is true of every cool-roof coating on the market. Rinsing the roof down periodically is maintenance worth doing, and the performance returns when you do.
Storage: keep the container tightly sealed, in the shade, dry, between 10 and 30°C. Keep it out of direct sun and away from heat.
