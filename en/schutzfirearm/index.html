Why SchutzFirearm

Oil does stop rust — but it also evaporates, migrates, and makes everything slippery. So we changed the approach: from an oil film to a ceramic one.

01

An actual ceramic film, not an oil film

Once cured, the liquid becomes a dense network of Si-N-Si and Si-O-Si bonded to the steel — not a layer of oil waiting to be wiped away or evaporate off.

02

Dry to the touch — grip improves

The usual trade for rust protection is a slick, oily gun. This cures dry. The surface goes matte, cuts glare, and racking the slide is easier than it was before.

03

Water, chemicals and heat

Water contact angle >100° — hydrophobic, so water beads up and rolls off instead of sitting there. Resistant to acids, alkalis, solvents and UV, and rated to 400°C once fully cured.

04

Barely there — and it goes a long way

4H pencil hardness at a thickness measured in microns: no dimensional change, no added friction. And because the film is that thin, 2–3 drops coat an entire firearm. One bottle lasts.

STRAIGHT TALK — what we owe you

Not a magic product —
just a chemistry nobody thought to put on a gun

2–3drops · a whole firearm · uses less than you'd believe

Straight talk before we sell you anything: this liquid was never developed for firearms. It belongs to the polysilazane family — a chemistry used industrially to coat specialty materials, jobs that demand a thin ceramic film with heat and chemical resistance well past what paint or oil can deliver. Which is exactly why almost nobody has heard of it: it was never sold as a consumer product.

Putting it on a firearm to stop rust is a small job for this chemistry. We didn't invent anything — we took something that already existed and pointed it at the right problem. And because it isn't magic, you get the limits too: it does not turn back time. It won't eat rust that's already there, it won't fill scratches you already have, and the chamfered front edge of the slide — the highest-wear spot on every pistol ever made — only gets partially disguised. What it does do is protect it well from here on.

Iron rule 1 — never apply it thick

This film works at a few microns. Thicker is not more protection — it just leaves streaks and hardens into something you'll have to fight off. Don't overdo it. Enough to coat is enough.

Iron rule 2 — you must wipe it off

Apply, wait 2–5 minutes, then wipe the excess off completely with a microfibre cloth. Forget to wipe and it becomes a real job to remove. Never leave it sitting wet.

Iron rule 3 — unsure? skip it

External surfaces are easy. But the bore and the internal slide components — if you aren't confident, leave them alone. Parts that live on tight tolerances are not worth the gamble.

How to use it

Everything you need is on this page. Honestly, the job is easy — you apply it and you wipe it off. It comes down to two things: keeping it thin and wiping on time. Get those right and it goes perfectly.

  1. Strip every trace of oil first

    The surface must be clean, dry and completely free of oil residue. If the gun has been oiled for rust protection, degrease it thoroughly first — a ceramic film cannot bond through oil. Skip this step and you've wasted the product.

    clean · dry · oil-free

  2. 2–3 drops onto the applicator — that's it

    Put 2–3 small drops onto the coating foam applicator, not directly onto the gun. And that is not a typo: 2–3 drops will cover the entire firearm. Consumption is remarkably low — a single bottle goes a very long way.

    2–3 drops = whole firearmonto the foam, not the gun

  3. Spread it out — just enough to coat

    Work it across the area you want covered. Enough to coat is enough. No second passes, no pressing, no saturating — overdoing it wastes product and makes the wipe-off harder. Tip: work one component at a time (slide / frame / small parts) and the timing is easier to manage.

    just enough to coatnever thick

  4. Flash time: 2–5 minutes — do not exceed

    This is the most important step of the whole job. Wait at least 2 minutes, but no more than 5. Past that, the film starts to set and removing the excess becomes very difficult. Time it — don't estimate.

    minimum 2 minutesmaximum 5 minutes

  5. Wipe off the excess — buff until it shines

    Gently remove the excess with a microfibre cloth, buffing in circular motions until the surface comes up glossy. Leave nothing behind or you'll get streaks. This step and the one before it are the entire job.

    microfibre clothcircular motionno residue

  6. Initial cure — 24 hours, keep it dry

    Let it set somewhere dry for at least 24 hours, and keep it away from water during that window. Store it somewhere well ventilated, not humid.

    24 h · no water

  7. Full cure — 7 days to full performance

    The film fully cures at 7 days, and only then delivers the complete specification — hardness, chemical resistance, heat rating. You can use the firearm normally in the meantime; it just isn't at 100% yet.

    full cure 7 days
⚠ The bore and internal slide components: if you are not confident, skip them entirely — parts that depend on tight tolerances aren't worth the risk. And once more: never leave the product sitting wet on the surface. Apply it, wipe it off on schedule, and that's the whole story.
Can you stop oiling it? The exterior no longer needs an oil film — that's the direct benefit here: no slick hands, and an easier grip when you rack the slide. But the internal mechanism still needs lubrication as normal. Use lubricant where the mechanism actually calls for it.

Coated steel, dropped in salt water

Salt water is the harshest ground any rust protection can be tested on — and it isn't remote at all. Human sweat is salt water, and it's the number one reason a carry gun starts rusting. So that's what we tested against.

Run it yourself: this is a simple accelerated test, not a standardised laboratory salt spray test — we'd rather say so plainly. What it does have going for it is that you can repeat it. Take two offcuts of steel, coat one, leave the other, drop them in salt water side by side. Seeing it with your own eyes beats taking anyone's word for it.
