STRAIGHT TALK — the truth up front

This one is genuinely hard and glossy —
but there are two things to know before you buy

2:1A : B BY WEIGHT — the discipline you can't skip

First: the color after cure — once set, the film is glossy and yellows heavily under accumulated sun. That's simply the nature of this heavy-duty chemistry family, and we won't dance around it: for this product we recommend always applying a topcoat, such as a PU floor paint — you get both looks and longer service life. And if it's timber you want in a clear finish that yellows very slowly, skip straight to our CrystalSeal instead (down the page).

Second: this is a two-component system — strength at this level is bought with mixing discipline. Weigh A:B exactly, stir until truly uniform, and apply in time. Do all three, and the film pays back every second you put in.

Badly mixed = never cures

Stir for at least 1 full minute until truly uniform — material that isn't mixed well enough will not cure. This is the number-one cause of failed jobs, not the product.

The clock runs 15 minutes

Once mixed, apply everything within 15 minutes — mix only what you can use, never the whole kit at once. Whatever you can't apply in time is money down the drain.

Yellows in the sun

Gorgeous gloss, but the amber tone deepens with accumulated sun — showpiece surfaces get a topcoat, timber that must stay clear gets CrystalSeal, and pure protection jobs can just let it yellow. Performance doesn't drop.

Thickness is durability's best friend

Want it tougher? Build the film thicker — especially exterior timber that has to take a beating: pile on extra coats, in exchange for accepting the yellowing.

How to use

Everything you need is on this page — the heart of any two-component system is weigh right, stir through, apply in time. Nail those three and nothing goes wrong.

  1. Prep the surface — clean, dry, minimal dust

    Always clean before coating. The surface must be completely dry with as little dust as possible — and the iron rule of every coating system: fill all cracks and holes flush first, then coat over. Skip this and no film, however good, can save you.

  2. Weigh A 2 parts : B 1 part (by weight)

    Use a scale, and mix only what you'll use — weighing examples: A 50 g : B 25 g / A 100 g : B 50 g / A 200 g : B 100 g

    A : B = 2 : 1Always weigh with a scale

  3. Stir until truly uniform — at least 1 minute

    Stir into one homogeneous mass, right into the base and sides of the container, for no less than 1 minute, then apply immediately — repeating because it matters most: poorly mixed material will not cure.

    Stir ≥ 1 minute

  4. Apply everything within 15 minutes

    Once mixed, the clock is running — use it all within 15 minutes. Never mix the whole kit at once; anything you can't apply in time is lost. If the mix is too viscous, you may add thinner up to 10% max.

    Pot life 15 minThinner ≤ 10%

  5. Apply — roller only if you want economy and a smooth finish

    We recommend a 4-inch foam or short-nap roller — you get a smooth glossy finish and the coverage in the table (a brush works, but uses noticeably more material) / Key trick: on surfaces that drink up the coating, like raw concrete or bare timber, apply a thin first coat to seal, then lay on the full second coat — otherwise the material vanishes into the substrate and your coverage collapses.

    Foam / short-nap roller 4"Absorbent surface: thin first coat1 kg ≈ 5 m² (2 coats)

  6. Recoat after about 6 hours — want tougher, add coats

    The next coat can go on after roughly 6 hours — and keep this principle: thickness is durability's best friend. Exterior work, timber, anything that must last: build extra coats for a thicker film.

    Recoat ~6 hTough job = thick film

  7. Wait 6+ hours — then back in service

    After the final coat, allow 6 hours or more before use — for immersion or heavy loads, the longer you let it set, the better.

    In service after 6 h+
⚠ Don't forget: the cured film is glossy and yellows heavily under accumulated sun — for this product we recommend always applying a topcoat, such as a PU floor paint / for a clear finish on timber that yellows very slowly, use CrystalSeal instead (details at the bottom of the page).
Storage: once opened, reseal tightly and keep away from air — good for approx. 3 more months.

SECOND ROLE — just as good at these

Two more roles: adhesion primer and moisture barrier

High grip + bonds to damp concrete + a continuous, 100% solids film with no pinholes — that combination makes Marine Guard equally at home as an adhesion-building primer and as a moisture barrier against rising damp.

Tile Floor Primer

Primer over glazed floor tile

Coating over floor tile is where paint peels most often — the glazed surface is too slick for ordinary coatings to grip. Use Marine Guard as the adhesion bridge before your finish system goes on.

Concrete Floor Primer

Primer before garage / factory floor paint

Apply before PU or industrial floor paint — seals the concrete, boosts adhesion, and because it bonds even to damp concrete, new slabs and humid areas keep the job moving with no long wait.

Moisture Barrier

Moisture barrier before floor coatings

Moisture rising from the slab is the classic cause of blistered, cracked, peeling floor paint — apply Marine Guard as the moisture barrier before your floor coating or covering / the key to this job: the film must be thick and continuous with zero pinholes — thickness is durability's best friend.

How to run these jobs: same product, same mix ratio for every role — as a primer, one thin coat over the whole area, wait 6 hours, then apply your finish system / as a moisture barrier, the opposite: lay it on thick and continuous, 2 coats minimum — wherever the film is thin or pinholed, moisture will find its way out / not sure your site qualifies?
