Why PatchPro

This product was never meant to be the star of the job — it is the step that has to come first so the star can actually do its work. Floor paint and waterproofing jobs that fail early almost never fail because of the paint or the liquid. They fail at the crack nobody closed properly.

01

Built for concrete floor cracks specifically

Not a general-purpose filler that also happens to work on floors — the bond strength is high and the cured material is genuinely strong. Once repaired, if the slab underneath is not moving hard, it will not crack again easily. That is the opposite of a cement patch, which usually reappears along the same line within a season or two.

02

The prep step before paint and waterproofing

Floor paint and waterproofing liquids are thin films, not fillers — run one across an open crack and it sags into the gap, ending up thinnest at exactly the point that needed to be thickest. Fill flush first, coat second. That is the order, and it is not negotiable.

We sell filler, and we are still not going to tell you it repairs every crack in the world — cracks come in types, and each type wants a different material. Read these five, and you will know for yourself whether your job wants this one or wants something else from our range.

Cement patching cement is a loop with no exit

Most people start with sand and cement because it is cheap and it is already on site. The problem is that fresh cement does not really bond to old cement — it just sits on top. Feather it thin and it cracks and lifts within months; lay it thick and you get an ugly ridge across the floor. Either way you are back at the same spot next year. If you have already patched the same line more than twice, the problem is not your technique. It is the material.

It is hard — which means it is not flexible

Strength is the whole point of this product, and the price of that strength is that it is not made for joints that move · Structural expansion joints, a slab that is still settling, timber that swells and shrinks with the weather, or a metal-sheet roof that moves every single day — none of those are its job. Roof laps and sheet joints go to SpackleFlex; awkward joints on fibre-cement board or slick surfaces go to DeepStick.

Very hard to sand — so finish it while it is soft

Once this material is fully cured, tidying it up afterwards is genuinely miserable work. Sandpaper barely touches it, you end up reaching for a grinder, and then you risk chewing into the sound concrete around the repair · Which means the most valuable time on this job is the thirty minutes while it is still soft — strike it flush, clean the edges, do it then. Do not tell yourself you will sand it later.

It works on walls, but it is not the best choice there

Put it on a wall and you will have no problem at all with strength. But walls want a filler that is slightly flexible and easy to sand and paint, and neither of those is this product's strong suit — for walls, FillerAce is the one built for the job. Choose by what is in front of you, not by which tub is already in the cupboard.

For genuinely heavy industrial work, we have something harder

Factory floors with forklifts running all day, road repairs, anywhere taking repeated impact — step up to PMMA, which is designed for heavy duty and sets so fast you can reopen the area within hours. You pay considerably more per kilo for that, and on the right job it is worth every baht.

How much do you need — work it out from the chase, not from floor area. This material is consumed by the volume of the gap you are filling, not by the size of the room · A quick estimate:

Grams ≈ width (mm) × depth (mm) × length (m) × 1.7

How to use it — all on this page

No separate manual to hunt down. The real numbers are all here.

  1. First, work out what kind of crack you have

    Shrinkage cracks, crazing across the surface, and shallow spalled patches where the top layer has broken away — those are its job · But a structural joint, a crack that keeps getting wider, or a slab that has dropped to a different level is not something to fill. Fix the cause first, or you will be patching the same line forever. Shrinkage crack = yesJoint that keeps moving = no

  2. Chase the crack open first — always

    This is the most important step on the page. A hairline crack that is simply smeared over will peel back out as a thin strip before long, because filler spread paper-thin has nothing to hold on to · Run a grinder or a cutting disc along the line and open it into a chase at least 5 mm wide and 5 mm deep, cut as a V or a U so the filler has real material to key into · Around spalled patches, knock away everything loose until you reach sound concrete. Hairlines must be chased openChase ≥ 5 mmRemove everything loose

  3. Clean the chase properly

    Work a wire brush along the groove to break out dust and loose grit, then vacuum it or blow it out — dust left sitting in the bottom is a layer that stops the filler from ever touching the concrete · Remove any oil or grease · And the chase must be dry before you fill. No standing water in the bottom. Wire brush + vacuumMust be dryNo oil

  4. Weigh out A : B = 2 : 1 by weight

    Actually weigh it. Do not judge it by eye — a ratio that is off gives you material that never reaches full hardness, and it will not show you that today. It will show you in six months · Mix hard for 2–3 minutes, scraping the bottom and the sides of the pot, until the colour is completely uniform with no streaks left (a mixing paddle on a slow drill is quicker and more thorough) · Mix only as much as you can use in about 30 minutes. Mixing the whole tub and then not keeping up means throwing the whole tub away. 2:1 by weightMix 2–3 minutesBatch for ~30 minutes

  5. Press it in — do not skim it over

    With a filling knife or a small trowel, press the material down into the chase until it is packed solid to the bottom, leaving no voids inside. A void you leave behind is the spot that sinks and cracks later · Fill flush or very slightly proud. Never leave it dished below the surface, because whatever you coat over the top will pool into that dip and show up as a line more obvious than the crack ever was.

  6. Finish it flat while the material is still soft

    As said above — this stuff is very hard to sand later. Strike the surface flush, clean up the edges, and wipe away anything that has gone outside the line while it is still soft · If you want a crisp edge and no mess on the surrounding floor, run masking tape along both sides of the chase before you fill, and pull it off before the material hardens — ten minutes of taping saves an hour on your knees with a grinder. Finish it now, not laterTape helps a lot

  7. Let it harden before you carry on

    Hard to the touch in about 6–8 hours · Wait a full 24 hours before floor paint or a waterproofing system goes over the top, and before the floor goes back into normal use · Cold or very humid weather means waiting longer. Nobody has ever been hurt by waiting. Touch-hard 6–8 hrsCoat over at 24 hrs

  8. Clean your tools with thinner immediately

    While it is still soft, thinner takes it off without much trouble — but let it harden on the blade and that tool is finished. You will not get it off. Putting the tools down and washing them at the end of the day does not work with this product.

The mistakes that waste the most material — never smear it thinly over a crack you have not chased open (the number one reason a repair lifts) / never guess the ratio by eye / never mix more than you can place in 30 minutes / never fill a chase that is still damp or full of dust / never thin it down to make it spread more easily — the strength you paid for goes out with the thinner.

Why cracks have to be closed before you coat

The question we get most often is "can't I just paint over it?" — you can, and inside a year you will watch the old line come back through the new paint. At that point you are not repairing one crack any more. You are redoing the whole floor.

A crack is not just untidy

A crack is a way in for water, and the water carries salts and moisture down to the reinforcing steel. The steel starts to rust, and rust takes up several times more volume than the steel it came from — so the crack slowly widens on its own, with nobody doing anything to it at all.

And on a floor about to be painted or waterproofed, the crack is always the place the coating fails first, because that is where the film ends up thinnest and where it gets pulled hardest.

Close the cracks, then decide how to finish

Concrete roof decks and rooftops that have to keep water out go on to SiliconePro (budget-friendly, handles standing water) or step up to Exotic if you want the full treatment · Interior floors, garages and driveways that just need to look good and stop shedding dust go to ThermaGlaze.

For floor repairs, this is the one we point to

Across our whole range, cracks and spalled patches in floors get one answer from us: PatchPro. Not because nothing else would work, but because this is the one built for that exact job, the easiest to use, and the cheapest of the ones that are actually strong enough.

Rooftops — every line closed before the coating goes on

An old roof deck crazed with cracks right across it is the job we see most often — and the way it is done is to work along and close every single line first, then coat the whole slab as one. Not to pour waterproofing over open cracks and hope.
