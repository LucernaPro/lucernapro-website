STRAIGHT TALK — what we say before you pay

It really is that tough — but there's a bill attached
4 things we'll tell you up front

1 kg≈ 1 SQM — THIRSTIEST IN THE HOUSE

One — it's thirsty. Using the pour-and-squeegee method (which gives the best film you can get), budget 1 kg per square metre. Switch to brush or roller and you'll cover more ground, but the film gets thinner — and a thinner film is less durable. That's physics, not a sales objection. Nothing is free.

Two — the smell is strong. Not "a bit of paint smell". Strong enough that you cannot work in a closed room: you need real airflow or a fan, always. If your site is an enclosed space or a small condo bathroom, we'll tell you honestly to buy something else rather than suffer through it.

Three — adhesion is good, not godlike. To be precise: on sound concrete it goes down directly with zero problems. But on tile or polished, sealed surfaces it needs a primer underneath — don't pour it onto a slick floor and hope for grip.

Four — it's too fast to work casually. The speed is what beats every other product we sell, and it's a double-edged sword: once the hardener powder is in, you have roughly 7–10 minutes in Thai site conditions. Mix only what you can squeegee out in that window. This is not a product for coating a bit, taking a phone call, coating a bit more — if you want a relaxed pace, see Exotic at the bottom of this page.

MIX RATIO

100 parts resin : 2% hardener powder by weight (1 kg resin = 20 g powder) — stir the resin first, scatter the powder in while stirring, then keep stirring 2–3 minutes until not a single grain is left. Mix in a metal pail: the reaction is exothermic.

PRIMER WE RECOMMEND

Tile / polished / slick surfaces → prime with Marine Guard first, then lay the PMMA over it (see Marine Guard →)

BUILT FOR FLAT WORK

This product is made for flat areas — roof decks, balconies, factory floors, car parks, walkways. For tall vertical work or complicated geometry we have better tools in the house; just ask.

How to use

Every number you need is on this page — with this product it all comes down to speed: have everything ready before you open the pail, then work in small bays you can actually finish.

  1. Prepare the surface: clean, dry, oil-free

    The substrate must be clean, dry and free of dust and oil — this goes onto concrete, metal and fibreglass, but every one of those has to be genuinely clean. Anything glossy or power-polished must be abraded dull first.

    clean · dry · oil-free

  2. Tile / polished surfaces → prime first

    Don't gamble. Slick surfaces you can't properly abrade get a coat of Marine Guard as primer first, then PMMA over the top. On sound concrete you can skip this step and go straight down.

    slick surface = Marine Guard first

  3. Fill every crack completely first

    House rule for every coating system we sell: cracks, spalled edges and potholes must be cut open, filled and levelled first — coating over a crack that's still hollow just books the failure for later. Use FillerAce for wall work or DeepStick for brutal joints, let it cure properly, then coat.

    fill first → then coat

  4. Lay out every tool before you open the pail

    Rubber squeegee or spreader, brush, roller, gloves, ventilation fan — all of it within reach first, because once that pail is open the clock moves faster than you expect.

    set up first, don't go hunting for tools

  5. Mix: 100 resin to 2% hardener powder

    Weigh it properly — 100 parts resin to 2 parts hardener powder by weight (1 kg resin = 20 g powder). Order matters: stir the resin to an even consistency first, then scatter the powder in while you're still stirring — never dump the powder in one heap and mix afterwards. Keep stirring for at least 2–3 minutes until there is not one undissolved grain left. One grain left behind is one spot where the film won't harden.

    100 : 2 by weightstir 2–3 min, zero grains leftmix in a metal pail

  6. Pour and squeegee — or brush it on

    Primary method (recommended): pour it onto the floor and squeegee it out evenly at 1 kg per square metre — this method guarantees every corner and low spot is covered, which is exactly why we like it for people who'd rather not be precious about technique.
    Secondary method: brush or roller for more coverage per kilo — good when budget is tight, but the film is thinner and less durable.

    pour & squeegee 1 kg / sqmbrush & roller = more area, thinner film

  7. The clock starts when the powder goes in — finish in 10 minutes

    This is the rule you cannot get wrong. Pot life at 20°C runs about 15 minutes — but on a Thai site in full sun with a substrate at 30–35°C, count on 7–10 minutes, and hotter means shorter. So: break the area into small bays · mix only what you can finish in 10 minutes · then mix a fresh pail. Mixing a big batch because you're tired of mixing is exactly how you end up with a pail of solid material to throw away.

    20°C ≈ 15 minhot site 30–35°C = 7–10 minsmall batches only

  8. Want a thicker build? Recoat within 2–4 hours

    For work that needs extra film thickness, apply a second coat — within 2–4 hours of the first. Leave it longer than that and abrade the surface lightly before recoating. The house principle stands: thickness is durability's best friend.

    recoat within 2–4 hrs

  9. Back in service in about 30 minutes

    This is where this product leaves the rest of the field behind — walkable in roughly 30 minutes, fully set in about 1–2 hours, depending on site temperature. Colder slows it slightly but it still cures, unlike ordinary systems that simply stop working.

    walkable ~30 minfully set 1–2 hrs
⚠ Mix in a metal pail only: the reaction of this system is exothermic — thin plastic or foam containers risk softening or melting. Use a metal pail and never leave mixed material standing in it.
⚠ Strong odour and flammable — read before opening: work in well-ventilated areas only, with a fan moving air. The material is flammable — absolutely no open flame, sparks, welding or grinding anywhere nearby. Wear gloves and safety glasses and avoid skin and eye contact.
Site temperature and storage: apply between 5–35°C — avoid anything colder or hotter / store in the original container, tightly sealed, in a cool, dry, shaded place away from direct sun and heat.
