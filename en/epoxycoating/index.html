STRAIGHT TALK — what has to be said before you pay

The finish will not be perfectly flat —
it follows the concrete your builder left, not the paint

2KTWO COMPONENT · mix A with B before use

This is the single biggest disappointment we see, so it comes before the sale: a roll-on floor coating (the trade calls it a coating) is a very thin film — about the thickness of a heavy house paint, nowhere near enough to hide an uneven slab. Waves, hollows, uneven trowelling, trowel marks, joints — they are all still visible after coating, and sometimes the gloss makes them more visible than before.

If you want a flat floor, fix the concrete, don't expect the paint to. Put simply: a floor coating's job is "durable and easy to clean", not "levelling".

So what about the mirror-flat floors in car showrooms and pharmaceutical plants? — That is a self-levelling system, a different system altogether, many times thicker and genuinely difficult to do. We sell both, but we will not sell you the roll-on kind while telling you it will come out flat — and if you choose self-levelling and don't know how, we teach you.

The secret of centimetre-thick floors

For anything several millimetres to a centimetre thick, nobody pours pure resin the whole way — it would cost a fortune, and a thick mass of resin generates heat from its own reaction (exotherm) until it yellows, scorches, shrinks and cracks. The real thing is a levelling layer trowelled first, then a thin finish over the top.

What goes in the levelling layer

Where there is chemical exposure, moisture pushing up from below, or heavy impact, use epoxy mortar (resin with graded quartz sand, trowelled) / but if the slab is fully dry and sees no chemicals, ordinary levelling or repair mortar is many times cheaper and perfectly adequate — don't let anyone sell you more than the job needs.

On slip resistance

Anti-slip aggregate can be added to the final coat, but to be plain it is "less slippery", not "non-slip" — a wet floor still needs care. Anyone promising 100% non-slip is selling a dream.

Always read the label

The figures on this page are for the standard formula — some formulas and some colours may differ. The label on the tin you receive is always the truth; mix according to that.

Colour — any shade made to order

How to choose a colour without regret

Don't trust the chart on your screen directly — open Google and type ral followed by the number from the chart, e.g. ral 6018. You'll see dozens of real examples of that colour on floors, walls and machinery — look at the real thing from several angles, then decide.

Why do it that way

Every phone and monitor shifts colour differently, and a colour on paper feels very different from the same colour across tens of square metres of floor — dark colours on a big floor look darker than you expect, light colours show marks more than you expect.

Colours people order most

Most factory floors go grey (easy on the eye, doesn't show grime) or green (contrasts with machinery, dropped items are easy to spot) / light, bright colours make a space feel brighter and show dirt sooner — which honestly means washing more often.

Any colour

How to apply

Every number is on this page — 80% of the job is preparing the slab, not painting it. Skip the prep and the floor peels within a year, every single time.

  1. Check the slab before you decide

    The slab must be truly dry (not just surface-dry — if moisture pushes up from below, the coating will blister, guaranteed) and sound enough, not crumbling to dust / A newly poured slab must cure a full 28 days before coating. Do not rush it.

    New slab = wait 28 daysDry all the way through

  2. Open the surface — the single most important step

    Grind the surface open with a grinder, diamond cup wheel or shot blasting so it is rough and porous enough for the coating to key into — everything must come off: polished trowelled skin, oil stains, old paint and loose concrete. Finish by vacuuming clean (sweeping is not enough; fine dust is what makes coatings peel).

    Grind open + vacuum

  3. Repair the slab full and flat first

    Cracks, holes, potholes — all must be filled and levelled before any coating, because a floor coating cannot hide them. Paint straight over a crack and it reappears through the film before long / For floor repairs we use PatchPro — cracks and holes alike; let it set fully, then coat over.

    Finish repairs with PatchPro first

  4. Prime (if the slab is dusty or absorbent)

    A slab that is still slightly dusty or very absorbent gets an adhesion primer first, to close the pores and lift the bond strength — we recommend Marine Guard as the primer for this job. It lets the coating bond at full strength and reduces air bubbles rising from the pores of the concrete.

    Porous / dusty slab = prime

  5. Mix A : B = 4 : 1 by weight

    The ratio for the standard formula, both Epoxy and PU, is 4 parts Part A : 1 part Part B by weight — weigh it, never eyeball it. A wrong ratio means a film that never sets, stays permanently tacky underfoot, or turns brittle and flakes — and there is no fix except tearing it out / Stir until it is genuinely uniform, one colour throughout the bucket, scraping the bottom and sides too. A lazy stir is the number-one cause of patchy floors.

    4 : 1 by weightWeigh, don't guessStir until truly uniform

  6. Mix only what you can use — the clock runs 30–40 minutes

    The reaction starts the moment you mix: pot life is about 30–40 minutes at 25°C — hotter weather makes it shorter. Past that the mix thickens until it won't spread, then hardens in the bucket. Mix only as much as you can apply in time; never mix the whole set at once.

    Pot life 30–40 min @25°C

  7. First coat

    Apply with a roller, brush or notched trowel, evenly and in one direction. Don't leave thick puddles — an over-thick film cures unevenly and can wrinkle / Cut in edges, corners and around column bases with a brush first, then roll the main floor.

    Touch-dry ~2 h

  8. Second coat — across the first

    Wait until the first coat is ready to recoat, 4–8 hours (cool or humid weather takes longer — overnight is fine), then apply the second coat at right angles to the first to close roller marks and thin spots — the second coat is not optional; the 50 m² coverage figure already assumes two coats.

    Recoat in 4–8 hSecond coat crosswise

  9. Let it cure before opening the floor

    Light foot traffic for finishing work after 5 hours, but wait 2 days before opening it for normal walking use · Vehicles, heavy goods and full chemical resistance need 7 days — the film is still hardening during this time; drive on it early and the tyre marks are permanent. Don't wash or mop the floor in the first week.

    Walking use 2 daysVehicles 7 days
⚠ Safety — this product contains solvent: the system contains solvent, so it has an odour and is flammable — work only where ventilation is genuinely good, keep an extraction fan running, no smoking or sparks in the area, and never work in a sealed room / Wear rubber gloves, safety glasses and an organic-vapour respirator — the hardener can cause skin sensitisation; on skin contact wash immediately with soap and water, and never clean your hands with thinner.
Clean tools with thinner immediately, while still wet — once hardened it never comes off; the roller is finished / Never add thinner to stretch the coating; a small amount to adjust viscosity only — dilute it heavily and the film is thinner and weaker, full stop / Store tins in a cool, dry, shaded place, lids tight, out of children's reach.
