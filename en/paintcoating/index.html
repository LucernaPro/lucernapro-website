Why painted surfaces can clean themselves

The same technology family as our Glass Coating — tuned for paint and coated metal panels, where the problem is "dust settles, rain streaks, sun yellows".

01

Rain washes it — no run-off streaks

A superhydrophilic film with a water contact angle under 10°: rain spreads into a thin sheet and runs off the whole panel, carrying loose dust with it. The dirty streaks under signs and along panel edges that make a building look old simply stop forming.

02

Clear — the original colour stays

The film is too thin to see; red, white and grey panels stay exactly the colour they were. On glossy dark paint a coat that's too thick will show a rainbow sheen, though — read Straight Talk point 1 before you decide.

03

Anti-static — dust struggles to settle

Paint and plastic build up charge and pull in dry dust. This film cuts static on the surface, so dust, exhaust soot and oil mist have a harder time sticking in the first place, well before the rain arrives.

04

Sunlight breaks down organic grime

The photocatalytic layer is activated by light and keeps breaking down oil, soot and carbon deposits on the surface. At fuel stations and roadside sites this is the main grime that yellows and blackens paint early.

How it works

A superhydrophilic surface does the opposite of a car ceramic coating — water doesn't bead and roll, it spreads into a thin sheet and drains off the whole surface, so it leaves no water spots and no run-off streaks.

Surfaces it suits

Aluminium composite panels (ACP), metal sheet and painted steel, train, bus and fleet paintwork, signs and light boxes, powder-coated finishes — any paint that is fully dried and cured.

What's in it

Nano-particles from the titanium dioxide, tantalum oxide and zirconium oxide families in a nano dispersion — an inorganic film that, by its nature, doesn't yellow in the sun the way organic films do.

Second mechanism — photocatalytic

Sunlight activates the layer to keep breaking down organic grime. Sun works by day, rain washes when it falls — two mechanisms working together for the life of the film.

Project work — the standard 6 steps

The same procedure the developer uses on high-speed rail and fuel-station work in China — our crew follows it on every project.

  1. Site survey and feasibility check

    Identify the surface (aluminium, painted steel, vehicle paint), the type of grime and the condition of the existing paint, then plan the job and site access.

  2. Test patch before the full job

    Coat a small corner of the project's real surface to confirm adhesion, clarity and the self-cleaning effect before committing to the whole area.

  3. Deep clean

    Wash with clean water, a neutral cleaner and soft tools to remove dust, oil and oil film completely. The surface must be free of residue.

  4. Apply the coating

    Once fully dry, apply by roller, spray or wipe in a thin, even layer, controlling the thickness so it neither runs nor misses.

  5. Natural cure, 4–6 hours

    Leave the film to set in air until it forms a dense, clear superhydrophilic layer on the paint.

  6. Inspect and hand over

    Check film uniformity, colour clarity and the self-cleaning effect across the whole surface before closing the job.

How to apply — everything on this page

Read to the end before opening the bottle — a clean surface and a thin coat are the two things that decide the result.

  1. Wash the paint clean and let it dry completely

    Remove all dust, oil, soot and old grime with a neutral cleaner. If there is grease or old wax, degrease first — paint that has been waxed or treated with a hydrophobic gloss coating will not accept this product.

  2. Apply a thin, even coat — thin is everything

    Large areas like panels and canopies: a short-nap roller (as in the site photos below) or an HVLP spray gun. Small jobs: a non-woven cloth. Never apply thick — an over-thick coat is what causes the rainbow sheen on dark paint, and it wastes product for no extra benefit.

    Short-nap rollerHVLP sprayNon-woven cloth

  3. Don't go back over the same spot

    The surface sets quickly — apply and leave it. Re-rolling or re-wiping once it starts to set will mark the film.

  4. Keep water off until the film has cured

    Water on the surface before the film cures leaves permanent marks that have to be washed off and recoated. Always check the rain forecast before outdoor work.

    Cure 4–6 h

  5. After 4–6 hours, done

    Fully cured at room temperature. From then on it can take rain and washing — clean with plain water or a neutral cleaner only.

⚠ Safety: The liquid contains solvent and is flammable — work in a ventilated area away from sparks and heat; wear gloves, goggles and an organic-vapour mask when spraying. Keep away from children and direct sunlight.
STRAIGHT TALK — what needs saying

Before you pay, read these 6 points —
this is not a miracle product, and we don't want you to misunderstand it

6things most sellers won't tell you
1 · Glossy dark paint + a thick coat = rainbow sheen

An over-thick film shows thin-film interference — an optical effect, not dirt, and it can't be wiped off. It's more visible on gloss black, dark blue and dark red than on light colours. Applying thin and correctly reduces it a great deal, but we don't guarantee it will be invisible — if you can't accept that, don't buy this product. Always test a small patch on the real paint first.

2 · This is not a car ceramic coating

Car ceramic coatings are hydrophobic — water beads and rolls away. This does the opposite: water spreads flat into a sheet. If you coat your own car expecting beading and deep gloss you'll be disappointed; for that go to Schutz Nano 9H. This product suits vehicles you can't keep washing — buses, fleet vehicles, train bodies — more than a family car.

3 · Water is the final carrier

Sunlight breaks organic grime down, but it's the sheet of water that carries dirt off the surface. Panels under an eave that rain never reaches mainly get the anti-static dust reduction. Bonded deposits like spray paint, tree sap or rust run-off won't come off by themselves — they still need wiping (just more easily).

4 · It won't repair paint that's already faded

The film is clear — paint that has faded, chalked or peeled won't come back. This product keeps good paint good for longer. If the paint is already gone, repaint first, then coat once the new paint has fully cured.

5 · The cure window is the danger zone

Water on the surface before the film cures = permanent marks that need washing off and redoing. Always check the forecast before outdoor work — this is the number-one cause of failed jobs with products of this type.

6 · Wrong care will ruin the film

After coating, wash with plain water or a neutral cleaner only. No close-range pressure washing, no stiff brushes, and no strong alkaline or acid stain removers — and never wax or gloss-coat over it, as that seals off the superhydrophilic surface.
