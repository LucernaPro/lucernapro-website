STRAIGHT TALK — the part others skip

The best binder in the world can't save you
if the stone is dirty or wet — this game is won at stone prep

1cmTHIN LAYER · STRONGER THAN CEMENT

A resin-bound system is binder coating every stone and welding them into one surface — so the entire bond lives on the stone's skin. If dust coats the stone, the binder grips dust instead of stone. If the stone is wet, moisture interferes with the cure — and a floor that should last a decade fails on day one.

So here's the straight talk before we sell you anything: wash the stone clean, then dry it completely. These two steps cost a little time and pay back more than anything else in the project. Don't rush, blend it thoroughly — and the strength of the binder? Leave that part to us.

Rule 1 — bone-dry stone

Wash the stone, then always let it dry completely before blending — wet stone is failure cause number one. No exceptions.

Rule 2 — no white stone

White stone turns yellow under the sun — we recommend golden-yellow and black washed-gravel tones. They stay beautiful for the long run.

Rule 3 — horizontal only

This model is designed for horizontal floors only — it cannot be trowelled vertically. Don't force it onto walls or upstands.

Durability trick

Want it tougher? Add more binder — more binder, more durability. Just don't push it to the point of pooling; past that it's pure waste, not extra strength.

How to use

Every number you need, right on this page — it comes down to two things: clean, bone-dry stone and mixing in batches, never all at once.

  1. Prepare the stone — the most important step

    Wash the stone clean — some batches carry a lot of dust and the binder can't work at full strength through it. Then let it dry completely. Wet stone is absolutely forbidden.

    Wash cleanDry completely

  2. Mix the binder A : B = 2 : 1

    Example: 1 kg of binder = Part A 666 ml : Part B 333 ml — always stir until fully uniform first, and mix only what you'll use. Never mix the whole kit at once: you won't keep up, and the binder will spoil in the bucket.

    A:B = 2:1Mix in batches

  3. Blend the stone — 1 kg binder per 20 kg stone

    Finish mixing the binder first, then add the stone gradually and blend until every piece is coated. No rush — smaller batches work too, e.g. 500 ml binder per 10 kg stone for easier trowelling. Add more binder for extra durability, just don't let it pool.

    1 kg binder : 20 kg stone

  4. Trowel it down within 15 minutes

    Each blended batch must be trowelled out within 15 minutes — spray the trowel with Sonax so it glides, lay to about 1 cm and level it flat and even. Don't leave blended gravel sitting in the sun.

    Place within 15 min~1 cm thickHorizontal only

  5. Topcoat (optional — not required)

    Want to push durability even further? Brush one more coat of binder over the finished surface, nice and unhurried — but skipping it is perfectly fine. The current formula is plenty strong on its own.

    Optional extra

  6. Clean the tools — before it cures

    Binder on your tools must be cleaned off with thinner immediately — and honestly: it's very hard to clean, borderline throw-the-tool-away hard. Use old or cheap tools for this job. Once cured, it's permanent. (Sonax is only for lubricating the trowel during work — it is not a cleaner.)

    Clean with thinner

  7. Let it set — then walk on it

    The floor starts setting at 4 hours after trowelling; leave it at least 24 hours and it's fully ready for use.

    Initial set 4 hrsReady in 24 hrs
⚠ Absolute no-gos: never use wet stone · never mix the whole kit at once — always work in batches · horizontal floors only, no vertical trowelling · no white stone, it yellows in the sun.
Storage: once opened, reseal unused binder airtight — no air contact — and it keeps for around 3 months.
